# Persona 01: Maria Gutierrez — Skeptical First-Timer

## Demographics

- **Age:** 34
- **Location:** Austin, TX (East Austin, renting a 2BR condo)
- **Occupation:** Senior Marketing Manager at a Series B SaaS startup (140 employees)
- **Income:** $112K/year + equity
- **Device:** iPhone 15 Pro (primary), work-issued MacBook Air (secondary, rarely used for personal stuff)
- **Browser:** Safari mobile, Chrome desktop

## Background

Maria grew up in San Antonio, first-generation college graduate (UT Austin, Communications). She's been in SaaS marketing for 9 years and is good at her job — she runs paid acquisition and lifecycle campaigns. She understands funnels, conversion, and messaging quality at a visceral level. This makes her a brutal evaluator of other products: she immediately notices when copy is vague, when a CTA is unclear, or when a landing page buries the value prop.

She lives with her partner (a nurse) and their dog. They have a combined household income of ~$190K but Austin rent eats a chunk of it. She has $28K in savings and contributes to a 401(k) but has no other investments. She's increasingly anxious about not building wealth outside her salary.

## How She Found AcreOS

Three weeks ago, her college friend Daniela posted an Instagram story showing a screenshot of a land sale profit — $4,200 on a parcel she'd held for 8 months. The caption said "passive income is real, DM me." Maria DM'd her. Daniela said she used "some app" to find the deal and sent a link to AcreOS. Maria saved the link and forgot about it for 11 days. She opened it last night at 10:47 PM while lying in bed.

## Technical Comfort Level

**High for consumer tools, zero for real estate.** Maria uses Canva (daily), Notion (workspace for her team), Slack (always open), Figma (reviews designs), Google Analytics, HubSpot, and Amplitude. She can navigate any well-designed SaaS product in minutes. She has never used a GIS tool, never looked at a county assessor website, and does not know what an APN is.

She does not read documentation. She does not watch tutorial videos. She expects the product to teach her through the interface itself — tooltips, contextual help, progressive disclosure. If she has to leave the app to understand the app, she's already annoyed.

## Expectations Shaped by Other Products

Maria's mental model for "good software" comes from Notion, Linear, and Canva. She expects:

- **Immediate clarity:** What does this product do? Who is it for? Can I use it right now?
- **Visual polish:** If the UI looks like a 2018 Bootstrap template, she assumes the company is underfunded or incompetent. Design quality = trustworthiness.
- **Fast time-to-value:** She wants to feel like she accomplished something within her first session. Not "signed up and saw a dashboard" — actually did a thing that moved her forward.
- **Mobile-first experience:** If the app feels like a shrunken desktop site on her phone, she'll close it. She doesn't pinch-to-zoom.
- **No jargon walls:** If a screen is full of terms she doesn't know (APN, assessed value, metes and bounds, legal description), she'll assume the product isn't for her.

She abandoned three SaaS products in the last month:
1. A budgeting app that required linking her bank account before showing any UI (trust barrier too early)
2. A habit tracker that had a 7-step onboarding wizard (too long, she skipped it and then couldn't figure out the app)
3. A meal planning app with no free tier (paywall before value)

## Goal for Using AcreOS

Maria doesn't have a clear goal yet. She has a vague aspiration: "Maybe I could buy cheap land and sell it for more." She needs AcreOS to sharpen that vague idea into a concrete next step. She's not ready to buy — she's ready to explore. She wants to understand:

- Is this a real thing or a scam?
- How much money would I actually need to start?
- What does the process look like end to end?
- Can this app hold my hand through it?

If AcreOS can make her feel like land investing is accessible, concrete, and not a black box, she'll stay. If it makes her feel stupid or overwhelmed, she'll leave and never come back.

## Realistic Failure Modes

1. **The 60-second bounce:** Maria opens AcreOS on her phone. The hero section says something about "empowering real estate professionals with AI-driven insights." She doesn't identify as a real estate professional. She's not sure what "AI-driven insights" means in practice. She scrolls once, sees a pricing page or a login wall, and closes the tab. Total time: 47 seconds.

2. **The jargon wall:** She signs up (free tier) and lands on a dashboard. She sees panels labeled "Parcel Pipeline," "Due Diligence Queue," and "Comps Analysis." She doesn't know what any of these mean. She clicks "Parcel Pipeline," sees an empty state that says "Add your first parcel." She doesn't have a parcel. She doesn't know how to find one. She closes the app.

3. **The trust gap:** She searches for land in Texas and AcreOS shows AI-generated valuations. There's no explanation of where the number comes from. She thinks, "Is this just making up numbers?" She doesn't trust it enough to act on it, and she doesn't trust it enough to keep exploring.

4. **The mobile breakage:** A key interaction (like a map view or a data table) doesn't work well on mobile. She has to scroll horizontally to see columns, or a modal doesn't fit the screen. She mutters "this is broken" and closes the app.

5. **The dead end:** She completes whatever the onboarding flow is, arrives at a state where the app is waiting for her to do something, and she doesn't know what that something is. No suggested next step, no guidance, no prompt. She stares at the screen, feels lost, and leaves.

## What Would Make Her Abandon

- **Paywall before value.** If she has to enter a credit card before she's seen what the product actually does, she's gone.
- **Desktop-only experience.** If the app clearly wasn't designed for mobile, she won't switch to her laptop. She'll just leave.
- **Unclear value proposition.** If after 60 seconds she still can't answer "what does this app do for me specifically," she's gone.
- **Feeling like an outsider.** If the language, design, or flow assumes she already knows real estate investing, she'll conclude "this isn't for beginners" and leave.
- **Slow load times.** If any page takes more than 3 seconds on LTE, she notices and gets frustrated.

## What Would Make Her Stay

- A clear, specific headline: "Find undervalued land, buy it, and sell it for profit — even if you've never invested before."
- An interactive first experience: "Let's look at land in your area" with a map she can explore immediately, no account required.
- Seeing a real example: a sample deal walkthrough showing "bought for X, sold for Y, here's how."
- A feeling of momentum: after 5 minutes, she should feel like she learned something real and could explain it to her partner.
- Contextual education: tooltips that say "APN = the parcel's ID number, like a Social Security number for land" instead of assuming she knows.

## Signature Quote

"I don't have time for this."

## Testing Priority

Maria tests the **first-touch experience, mobile responsiveness, onboarding clarity, and jargon accessibility.** If AcreOS can convert Maria from "curious skeptic" to "engaged explorer" in one session, the product's entry funnel is working.
