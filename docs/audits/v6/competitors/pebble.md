## 5. Core Workflows

**Pebble labels their end-to-end flow as "How Deals Get Done":**

1. **Property -> Campaign**: Upload property records, then launch marketing campaigns (direct mail with satellite imagery, cold calling, texting, ringless voicemail). Track response rates and ROAS.

2. **Campaign -> Communication**: Capture every inbound reply in a unified inbox. Make/receive calls, send texts, and manage email from one screen. AI transcribes and summarizes calls automatically.

3. **Communication -> Deal**: Qualify leads and create deal opportunities. Move deals through a visual Deal Board with drag-and-drop stages. Attach notes, documents, and tasks.

4. **Deal -> Close**: Generate documents from property data, manage due diligence with property maps, assign tasks to team members, and track KPIs to close.

5. **Auto Followup Loop**: Configure action plans that automatically re-engage non-responsive leads via sequences of texts, emails, call reminders, and direct mail pieces.

## 7. What Pebble Communicates Clearly That AcreOS Doesn't

1. **The "messy to organized" transformation narrative.** Their secondary landing page opens with "Land investing is messy." This names the pain before pitching the solution. It validates the prospect's current frustration.

2. **An explicit end-to-end deal lifecycle visual.** "How Deals Get Done" walks through Property -> Campaign -> Communication -> Deal as a labeled pipeline. The user immediately understands where they enter the workflow and where they exit.

3. **A genuine free tier.** $0/month with 2,000 property records and unlimited campaigns. This is a real product experience, not a 14-day trial. The prospect can close a deal before paying anything.

4. **Land-specific vocabulary throughout.** Every term references parcels, acreage, land sellers, land deals. The platform never tries to be a general real estate tool. The positioning is unambiguous.

5. **Satellite imagery as a built-in feature.** "Every mailer can include the actual satellite image of that property, automatically pulled from GPS coordinates, at no extra charge." This is a tangible, visual differentiator shown directly in marketing materials.

---

## 8. Transferable Patterns AcreOS Should Adopt

1. **Name the chaos, then offer the system.** "Land investing is messy" is a masterclass in empathy-first positioning. AcreOS should consider an equivalent opening that validates the operational complexity real estate professionals face before presenting the platform.

2. **Visual deal lifecycle mapping on the homepage.** The "Property -> Campaign -> Communication -> Deal" flow gives prospects immediate mental model clarity. AcreOS should show its own end-to-end workflow as a visual pipeline on the landing page.

3. **Free tier with real utility.** Pebble's free plan includes unlimited campaigns and direct mailing with a 2,000-record cap. This lowers the barrier to first-deal completion. AcreOS should evaluate whether a constrained-but-functional free tier would accelerate adoption.

4. **Auto followup sequences as a first-class feature.** Pebble labels "Auto Followups" as a discrete, named capability, not buried in settings. The combination of text + email + call reminder + direct mail in one sequence is prominently marketed.

5. **AI call summaries as a default behavior.** "All recorded calls and voicemails are automatically transcribed, with AI-generated summaries" -- this is positioned as something that just happens, not an opt-in feature. AcreOS should consider making AI-assisted features feel automatic rather than requiring configuration.

6. **Minimal top-nav with content-driven discovery.** Pebble's three-item nav (Pricing, Login, Book a Demo) forces the homepage itself to be the feature tour. This keeps bounce-prone visitors engaged in a single scroll rather than fragmenting attention across subpages.
