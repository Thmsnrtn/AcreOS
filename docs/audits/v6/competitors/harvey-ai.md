# Harvey AI — Aspirational Reference Dossier

**URL:** harvey.ai
**Category:** AI-native legal platform (NOT a competitor)
**Purpose of this dossier:** Study how Harvey communicates AI-native capabilities to a professional audience that never had them before, and extract patterns AcreOS can use to explain agent-based automation to skeptical real estate professionals.

**Company facts:**
- Founded: Summer 2022 by Winston Weinberg (ex-litigator, O'Melveny & Myers) and Gabriel Pereyra (ex-Google DeepMind, Meta)
- Revenue: ~$190M (2025)
- Valuation: $11B (March 2026)
- Users: 100,000+ legal professionals across 1,000+ organizations in 60+ countries
- Funding path: $5M seed (OpenAI) -> $23M A (Sequoia) -> $80M B -> $100M C -> $300M D -> $300M E -> $160M F -> $200M at $11B

## 1. The Five-Second Test

**What Harvey's landing page communicates in five seconds:**

**Headline:** "Practice Made Perfect"

**Subheadline:** "Today's top law firms and in-house legal teams trust Harvey to elevate their craft and navigate complexity."

**CTA:** "Request a Demo"

**Banner:** "Harvey Agents execute legal work end-to-end"

**What registers instantly:**
1. This is for lawyers (not general knowledge workers)
2. The best firms already use it (social proof embedded in subheadline: "top law firms")
3. It elevates existing expertise rather than replacing it ("elevate their craft")
4. It handles complexity (the core pain of legal work)

**AcreOS lesson:** Harvey does NOT lead with technology. No mention of AI, LLMs, or automation in the headline. The word "AI" appears nowhere above the fold. Instead, they lead with the *outcome* — perfected practice. AcreOS should consider leading with what the professional becomes, not what the technology does.

## 2. How Harvey Explains AI to Lawyers

### Pattern 1: Outcome-First, Technology-Second

Harvey's platform page headline:
> "Engineered for Every Task"

Platform value proposition:
> "More than 100,000 legal professionals around the world use Harvey to cut through complexity to navigate complex legal work."

They name the AI assistant:
> "An Intelligent Legal Coworker"

Not "AI assistant" or "chatbot" or "language model." A **coworker**. This anthropomorphizes the technology into a relatable work relationship.

> "Research, reason, deliver. Get instant answers, comprehensive reasoning, and polished drafts in one unified interface."

**AcreOS lesson:** "Coworker" is a masterful frame. It implies the AI works alongside you, not for you or instead of you. AcreOS should consider naming its AI capabilities in relational terms — not "AcreOS AI" but something that implies a working partnership.

### Pattern 2: Verbs, Not Nouns

Harvey describes capabilities as actions, not features:

> "Ask questions, analyze documents, and draft faster with domain-specific AI."

> "Securely store, organize, and bulk-analyze legal documents."

> "Research complex legal, regulatory, and tax questions across domains."

> "Run pre-built Workflow agents or build your own, tailored to your firm's needs."

> "Access Harvey where you already work and ground every answer in sources you trust."

Every product description starts with a verb. The user is the subject. The AI is the tool.

**AcreOS lesson:** Feature descriptions should be verb-first, user-as-subject. Not "AI-powered data enrichment" but "Research any parcel and get county records, ownership history, and comparable sales in seconds."

### Pattern 3: The "Bounce Ideas" Frame

For skeptical professionals who fear AI will get things wrong:
> "Bounce ideas off an intelligent partner available 24/7 to pressure-test your thinking."

This is genius positioning. It doesn't claim AI is always right. It positions AI as a thinking partner you can spar with. The professional retains authority.

**AcreOS lesson:** For skeptical real estate professionals, AcreOS could frame AI as "pressure-test your comps" or "sanity-check your offer price" — putting the professional's judgment first and AI as the sounding board.

### Pattern 4: Citations = Trust

> "Citations make it easy to verify accuracy before you share or act."

> "Ground every answer in sources you trust."

Harvey repeatedly emphasizes that AI outputs are sourced and verifiable. This directly addresses the #1 fear of lawyers using AI: hallucination.

**AcreOS lesson:** Every AI-generated data point in AcreOS should have a visible provenance. "Source: Pima County Assessor, retrieved April 2026" is trust-building in the same way citations are for lawyers.

## 3. How Harvey Explains Agents

This is the most relevant section for AcreOS.

**Headline:**
> "Harvey Agents execute legal work end-to-end, so you can focus on what only lawyers can do."

**Key framing:** Agents do the work "so you can focus on what only [professionals] can do." This positions automation as liberating, not threatening. It implies there IS work only the professional can do — the AI handles the rest.

**Creation language:**
> "Quickly create customized Workflow agents with natural language prompts"

> "Ask Harvey to automatically optimize custom Workflow agents for faster performance."

**Control language:**
> "Permissions and governance controls"

> "Manage Workflow agent access and granular editing permissions based on roles and teams."

**Scale proof:**
> "25,000+ custom agentic workflows built in Harvey"

**Customer results:**
> "One recent workflow reduced the time taken to produce lease summaries from three to four hours to three to four minutes." — Ashurst

> "75% time savings on due diligence with tailored custom workflows at GSK Stockmann"

> "800+ hours saved by Carvana with structured litigation workflows"

**Workflow types listed:**
- Litigation prep (summarize complaints, prepare arguments)
- Due diligence (extract key contract terms)
- Document production (embed firm style guides)
- Stakeholder updates (regulatory change distillation)

**AcreOS lesson:** Harvey avoids technical AI terminology entirely. No "agentic AI," no "autonomous agents," no "LLM pipelines." They say "workflow agents" — which sounds like a workflow that works for you, not a robot making decisions. AcreOS should study this vocabulary carefully:

| Harvey Says | Harvey Does NOT Say |
|---|---|
| Workflow agents | Autonomous AI agents |
| Execute work end-to-end | Agentic automation |
| Customized with natural language | Prompt engineering |
| Permissions and governance | Guardrails |
| Optimize for faster performance | Fine-tuning |

## 4. Trust Architecture

### Security as a First-Class Concern

Harvey has a dedicated `/security` page with this headline:
> "Secure legal AI for the most sensitive matters"

> "Harvey keeps your client data safe with world-class security and data privacy measures."

Six pillars, each addressing a specific lawyer fear:

**1. Dedicated Security Expertise:**
> "Harvey's in-house security team spans infrastructure, product, and operations. We implement robust security protections across the stack, with 24/7 coverage and end-to-end monitoring."

**2. Data Sovereignty:**
> "With Harvey, you retain full control over your data. Decide which data to upload, set retention policies, delete data anytime, and keep everything in-region—EU or Switzerland, US, or Australia."

**3. No Model Training (the big one):**
> "Harvey contractually guarantees through our Platform Agreement that your data stays yours. We don't use inputs, outputs, or uploaded documents to train underlying models."

**4. Enterprise Features:**
> "Harvey includes default controls that enterprise teams expect: SAML SSO, audit logs, IP allow-listing, data lifecycle management, and more."

**5. Enforceable Commitments:**
> "Harvey's Security Addendum includes binding terms on data protection, data access, incident response SLAs, and other controls aligned with SOC 2, ISO, GDPR and other standards."

**6. Independently Tested:**
> "Harvey partners with top-tier security firms, including Schellman, NCC Group and Bishop Fox, to perform in-depth audits."

Certifications prominently displayed: SOC 2 Type II, CCPA, ISO 27001, GDPR.

**AcreOS lesson:** Real estate professionals handling land transactions have real data sensitivity concerns (purchase prices, seller motivation, ownership info). AcreOS should have a clear, simple security posture page. The "no model training" guarantee is especially important — AcreOS should explicitly state that customer data is never used for model training.

## 9. Design Patterns for Explaining AI to Skeptics

### Pattern A: Lead with the professional's identity
Harvey's headline is "Practice Made Perfect" — it's about the lawyer's practice, not the tool. The professional's identity is the hero.

### Pattern B: Social proof in the subheadline
"Today's **top** law firms..." — before the visitor even scrolls, they know the elite already use this.

### Pattern C: Separate "what it does" from "how it works"
Harvey's landing page explains zero technical details. The platform page explains what each product does (verbs). Only the blog discusses how it works.

### Pattern D: Quantify the mundane
"Three to four hours to three to four minutes" for lease summaries. The before/after on a boring task is more persuasive than any feature list.

### Pattern E: Name the work, not the tech
"Due Diligence," "Contract Analysis," "Legal Research" — these are job activities, not feature names. Lawyers see their own work reflected back.

### Pattern F: Control language for governance-sensitive buyers
"Permissions," "governance controls," "granular editing permissions," "roles and teams" — for buyers who need to explain AI adoption to partners or boards, Harvey gives them the vocabulary of control.

### Pattern G: ROI Calculator as conversion tool
Two interactive ROI calculators let prospects self-serve the business case before talking to sales.

### Pattern H: "92% monthly usage" as the killer metric
Most SaaS tools have terrible adoption. Harvey leads with usage rate, which says "people actually use this daily" — the strongest possible signal that the product delivers value.

## 10. What AcreOS Should Steal

### S1: Outcome-first headline
Replace any technology-forward copy with professional-identity copy. Not "AI-powered land investing platform" but something that reflects what the real estate professional becomes.

### S2: Verb-first feature descriptions
Every feature card should start with what the user does, not what the technology is. "Research any parcel" not "AI data enrichment engine."

### S3: The "Intelligent Coworker" frame
Position AI as a team member, not a tool. "Your research analyst who never sleeps" is more relatable than "automated data pipeline."

### S4: Source attribution on all AI outputs
Every AI-generated insight should show its source. This is table stakes for professional trust.

### S5: Security as top-level navigation
If AcreOS handles sensitive deal data, security posture should be visible, not buried.

### S6: Before/after time metrics
"County research that took 4 hours now takes 30 seconds" is more persuasive than any feature list.

### S7: Usage metrics as social proof
If AcreOS can show "X queries per day" or "Y parcels researched this month" — usage proves value better than testimonials.

### S8: Solutions organized by workflow, not by feature
Real estate professionals think in workflows: "I'm doing due diligence" or "I'm running comps." Navigation should mirror their mental model.

### S9: No jargon discipline
Never say LLM, neural network, machine learning, or "powered by GPT" in user-facing copy. Say what it does in the professional's language.

### S10: ROI calculator
An interactive tool where a prospect enters "hours per week on county research" and sees projected time savings would be a powerful conversion asset.

## 11. The Meta-Lesson

Harvey's $11B valuation comes from a core insight: **lawyers didn't need AI explained to them — they needed their work explained back to them with AI already embedded.**

Harvey never asks lawyers to learn a new category. It shows lawyers their existing workflows (research, drafting, due diligence, review) running faster and better. The AI is invisible infrastructure, not a feature.

AcreOS should apply the same principle: don't ask real estate professionals to understand AI. Show them their existing workflows (parcel research, lead qualification, offer generation, due diligence) running at a speed and quality they didn't think was possible. The AI is the engine, not the hood ornament.
