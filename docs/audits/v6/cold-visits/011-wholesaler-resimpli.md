# Cold Visitor Simulation 011: Wholesaler (REsimpli User)

**Visitor profile:** Wholesaler, 5 years experience. Primarily does house wholesaling but has dabbled in land (3-4 land deals in the last year). Uses REsimpli currently at $299/mo. Looking for something cheaper that handles both land and house deals. Team of 1. Source: direct URL from a land investing Facebook group.

**Date:** 2026-04-18 (re-score round)
**Pages visited:** Landing, Pricing
**Source URL:** https://acreos.fly.dev/ and https://acreos.fly.dev/pricing
**Landing page version:** v6 (post-headline change, post-badge removal)

---

## Question 1: CATEGORY (60 seconds on landing page)

**Score: 4/5**

### What this visitor sees

Headline: **"The AI-Powered Platform for Land Investors."** Subhead: *"Find motivated sellers. Analyze parcels. Send direct mail. Close deals. All in one platform."* Then a "How AcreOS Works" section with 4 steps: Import or Find Parcels, AI Analyzes Each Parcel, Launch Direct Mail Campaigns, Close Deals & Track Returns.

### What the visitor says out loud

> "The AI-Powered Platform for Land Investors. Okay, crystal clear. This is a land tool. Not a general real estate tool. Not a wholesaling CRM. It's for land."

> "I do land deals but I'm primarily a house wholesaler. So the question is: does this also handle house deals, or is it land-only?"

> "The subhead says 'Find motivated sellers. Analyze parcels. Send direct mail. Close deals.' Parcels. That's land language. House wholesalers say 'properties' or 'houses,' not 'parcels.' Direct mail -- I do direct mail for houses too, but the framing here is clearly land-first."

> "How AcreOS Works: Step 1, Import or Find Parcels. Step 2, AI Analyzes Each Parcel. Step 3, Launch Direct Mail Campaigns. Step 4, Close Deals & Track Returns. This is a land acquisition funnel, top to bottom."

> "Features: Portfolio Mapping with 'property boundaries, flood zones, and zoning data' -- that's land. AI Valuations with 'comps analysis and 18 open data sources' -- land comps, not house ARV. AI Deal Intelligence -- 'Sophie scores leads, drafts offers, and surfaces the best opportunities.' That could work for either."

> "I know immediately what this is. The old version of this page apparently said 'real estate professionals' -- if it had said that, I might have thought this could handle my house deals too. But 'Land Investors' is unambiguous. I am partly a land investor, but mostly I'm a house wholesaler. This might work for my land side."

### Assessment

The new headline does exactly what it is supposed to do: immediate category identification. Within 5 seconds, this visitor knows AcreOS is a land investing platform. The word "parcels" in the subhead and all four "How It Works" steps reinforces it. For this visitor -- a hybrid wholesaler who does some land -- the specificity is a double-edged sword. It correctly identifies the product category (4/5) but also tells the visitor that their primary business (house wholesaling) is not served. The old "real estate professionals" headline would have scored lower on category clarity but higher on perceived applicability for this visitor.

## Question 2: VALUE PROP (next 60 seconds)

**Score: 3/5**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "Everything you need to run your land business. Okay, 'land business.' Not 'real estate business.' Got it."

> "'Portfolio Mapping -- Visualize every parcel on interactive maps with property boundaries, flood zones, and zoning data.' For my land deals, this is great. I currently use LandGlide for parcel maps. For my house deals, I don't need parcel boundaries -- I need street view and ARV comps."

> "'AI Valuations -- Instant property valuations powered by comps analysis and 18 open data sources.' For land, this is gold. REsimpli has ARV for houses but nothing for land comps. But can it also do ARV for houses? The page doesn't say."

> "'Campaign Automation -- Multi-channel outreach with SMS, email, and direct mail sequences.' This is table stakes. REsimpli does this too. But if AcreOS only targets land campaigns and doesn't have house-specific templates, that's a gap for me."

> "'Sophie, your AI copilot, scores leads, drafts offers, and surfaces the best opportunities.' If Sophie understands land deals, that's unique. REsimpli's AI is house-focused. But does Sophie also understand house deals? Nothing on this page suggests she does."

> "Social proof: 18 free data sources, $0 to get started, 14-day free trial, 500+ properties managed. Fine, but 500 properties is small. My REsimpli account alone has over 2,000 property records."

> "Bottom line: the value prop is strong for my land deals but unclear for my house deals. And I do 4x more house deals than land deals. If I switch, I need a platform that handles both, or I'd need to run AcreOS AND REsimpli, which defeats the purpose."

### Assessment

The value proposition lands cleanly for the land-investing subset of this visitor's business. But because the visitor is primarily a house wholesaler, half the value prop falls into uncertainty. The landing page makes no claim about handling house deals -- which is honest positioning -- but it means this visitor sees AcreOS as a potential supplement rather than a replacement. REsimpli, for all its limitations on land, at least handles the house side. Score reflects strong land value (4/5 partial) dragged down by the gap on the visitor's primary business (2/5 partial).

## Question 3: PRICING (2 minutes)

**Score: 4/5**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "I pay $299/mo for REsimpli Pro. Let me see what AcreOS costs."

> "Free: $0, 10 leads, 3 properties. Starter: $20/mo. Pro: $49/mo. Scale: $79/mo. Okay, this is dramatically cheaper than REsimpli."

> "Unless... I could use AcreOS for land and downgrade REsimpli to a cheaper plan since I'd be offloading the land side. Let me think about that."

> "BYOK Data Providers on Pro and Scale. Interesting -- I can bring my own API keys. REsimpli charges per lookup on top of subscription."

> "The pricing structure itself is excellent. The question is not whether it's affordable -- it's whether it replaces enough of REsimpli to justify running two tools or dropping REsimpli entirely."

### Assessment

The pricing is clear, well-structured, and dramatically cheaper than REsimpli. The visitor can immediately do the comparison math. The issue is not the price -- it is whether the visitor can justify the tool as a standalone (they cannot, because their primary business is house wholesaling). For the land-only portion of their business, the pricing is a home run. The annual toggle and "Save 20%" badge are clean touches. Feature comparison table does its job well.

**Yes, for the free tier, to evaluate for land deals only.**

> "I'll sign up for Free to test it with my land deals. 10 leads and 3 properties is enough to kick the tires."

> "My hesitations:"

> "Three: The landing page is focused on acquisition -- 'Find motivated sellers, analyze parcels, send direct mail.' But what about disposition? I flip land. I need to list it for sale, track buyer inquiries, and manage closings on the sell side too."

> "Four: The 500+ properties managed stat is low. REsimpli has tens of thousands of users. AcreOS is young."

> "I'll try it. Free tier. No commitment. See if the mapping and AI valuation features are as good as they look."

### Unanswered questions

- Can AcreOS handle house deals at all, or is it strictly land?
- Is there CSV import for migrating leads?
- Does the platform handle disposition (selling properties), not just acquisition?
- Is there a mobile app for checking deals in the field?
- Can I integrate AcreOS data with REsimpli (Zapier, API)?

### Hesitations

- Primary business (house wholesaling) is not served
- Small user base / early platform
- No visibility into disposition workflow from the landing page
- Running two CRMs is operationally messy

The visitor finds the disposition workflow (Listings, Marketplace) that was missing from the landing page messaging. For land deals specifically, this first-run experience is strong. The Blind Offer Wizard is a differentiated feature. The visitor's only disappointment is the confirmed absence of house-specific tools, which they already expected from the landing page.
