## Question 3: PRICING (2 minutes)

**Score: 4/5**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "I'm building this on the side with limited budget. Let me see pricing."

> "Free: $0, 10 leads, 3 properties. Okay. That's enough to start. I'm not managing hundreds of leads yet -- I'm trying to get my first deal."

> "Starter: $20/mo. That's a Netflix subscription. 250 leads, 50 properties, campaigns. Once I start sending mail, I'd probably need this."

> "Pro: $49/mo. 500 leads, BYOK data providers. I don't know what BYOK means. 'Bring your own key'? Key for what? Data providers? I don't have any data provider accounts."

> "Scale: $79/mo. 10 seats, unlimited everything. Way more than I need."

> "The feature comparison: AI Deal Intelligence is included on Free. Document Generation -- Free. Portfolio Mapping -- Free. Okay, so the AI features I want are all on the free tier. Campaigns are not -- I need Starter for that. SMS/Voice is only Pro and above."

> "Realistically: I start Free. I learn the platform. When I'm ready to send my first mail campaign, I upgrade to Starter at $20/mo. That's doable."

> "$20/mo is cheaper than the land investing courses I've been watching, which cost $500-2,000. If this tool teaches me how to do deals by walking me through the process, it's worth more than a course."

> "14-day free trial on paid plans. So when I'm ready to try campaigns, I get two free weeks. Smart."

> "One concern: the pricing page doesn't explain what 'Leads,' 'Properties,' and 'Notes' mean in context. As a beginner, I'm not sure what counts as a 'lead' vs a 'property' vs a 'note.' Is a lead a person? Is a property a parcel? What's a note -- a seller-financed note? I learned about those but haven't done one."

### Assessment

The pricing works well for this visitor because the free tier includes the AI features that attracted them. The upgrade path is clear and affordable: Free -> Starter ($20/mo) when ready for campaigns. The pricing is not intimidating. The gap is terminology: a beginner may not understand what "Leads," "Properties," and "Notes" mean in the context of this platform. Adding brief descriptions (e.g., "Leads = seller contacts," "Properties = parcels you're evaluating," "Notes = seller-financed deals") would help beginners. The "BYOK Data Providers" label is meaningless to this visitor.

'USDA + Census county intelligence.' That shows county data but I'm not sure how to get from county data to a list of parcels."

> "Skip Tracing -- 'Find owner contact info.' I don't have any owners to trace yet. I need to find owners first."

> "AI Hub -- let me ask Sophie for help. 'How do I get started as a new land investor?' If Sophie can guide me through the first steps, that would solve a lot."

> "Blind Offer Wizard -- 'Calculate blind offers step-by-step.' This sounds useful but I need leads first before I can send blind offers."

> "Intelligence section: Land Credit, Acq. Radar, Document Intel, Cohort Retention, Capital Mkts. I don't know what most of these do. Some of these sound like enterprise features. I'm one person trying to do my first deal."

### Assessment

The first-run experience is the weakest point for this visitor. The gap between the landing page's clean four-step narrative and the product's 30+ feature sidebar is jarring. A complete beginner with no CRM experience, no existing data, and no leads faces a cold start problem: the product is designed for someone who already has data to import, leads to manage, and deals to track. Without an onboarding wizard, getting-started guide, or progressive feature reveal, this visitor is likely to click around aimlessly, feel overwhelmed, and possibly churn before ever using the AI features that attracted them. The AI Hub (Sophie) could partially solve this if Sophie can serve as an interactive onboarding guide, but the visitor has to discover that on their own.

## Summary

| Question | Score | Notes |
|----------|-------|-------|
| 1. Category | 5/5 | Perfect match to Google search; headline answers the query directly |
| 2. Value Prop | 4/5 | AI features address beginner anxiety; slightly overwhelming feature breadth |
| 3. Pricing | 4/5 | Free tier with AI is compelling; terminology unclear for beginners |
| 4. Signup Readiness | 4/5 | Signing up immediately; $0 risk; analysis paralysis is the only barrier |
| 5. First-Run | 3/5 | Feature overload; no onboarding; cold start with zero data |

**Overall: 4.0/5** -- The landing page does an excellent job of attracting this visitor and matching their search intent. The headline "The AI-Powered Platform for Land Investors" is a direct answer to their Google query. The "How It Works" section mirrors the workflow they've been learning about on YouTube, creating instant recognition and trust. The free tier with AI included removes the budget barrier.

The drop-off happens post-signup. The product's feature density is designed for intermediate-to-advanced operators who know their workflow. A complete beginner needs hand-holding: "Here's your first step. Click here. Now do this." Without onboarding, this visitor is at high risk of signing up, getting overwhelmed, and never returning.

**Recommendation for this persona:** Add a simple onboarding flow for new users with zero data: (1) Pick your first target county, (2) Import or search for parcels, (3) Run AI valuations on your first parcel, (4) Generate your first blind offer letter. This would bridge the gap between the landing page's four-step promise and the product's reality.

**Comparison to Visitor 010 (initial round, also beginner):** Visitor 010 (YouTube beginner) scored 3.4/5 with the old landing page. Visitor 013 scores 4.0/5 -- a +0.6 improvement. The gains are entirely in Category (5/5 vs 3/5, because the old headline said "real estate professionals" which confused a beginner who only knew about land) and Signup Readiness (4/5 vs 3/5, because "Public Beta" removal and clearer positioning reduce hesitation). First-Run remains the weak point for both beginner visitors.
