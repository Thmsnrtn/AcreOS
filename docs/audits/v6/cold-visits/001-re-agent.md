# Cold Visitor Simulation 001: Real Estate Agent

**Visitor profile:** Licensed real estate agent (residential), uses Zillow and MLS daily, never heard of "land investing" as a category. Looking for a CRM to manage buyer/seller clients.

**Date:** 2026-04-18
**Pages visited:** Landing, Pricing, Auth

---

## Question 1: CATEGORY (60 seconds on landing page)

**Score: 2/5**

### What this visitor sees

The headline reads: **"The operating system for real estate professionals"** -- the word "professionals" is broad enough to include them. The subhead says: *"AcreOS replaces your spreadsheets, disconnected tools, and guesswork with a single platform that manages leads, automates outreach, and closes deals -- powered by AI."*

Then the strategy badges appear: **Wholesaling, Fix & Flip, Buy & Hold, STR / Airbnb, Land, Multifamily, Commercial, Creative Finance, Notes.**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "Operating system for real estate professionals -- okay, that could be me. Manages leads, automates outreach, closes deals. Sounds like a CRM."

> "Wait -- Wholesaling? Fix & Flip? Creative Finance? Notes? These are investor categories, not agent categories. Where's 'buyer representation'? Where's 'listing agent'? Where's 'broker'?"

> "I don't see MLS integration mentioned. I don't see transaction coordination. I don't see showing scheduling. This isn't for agents."

> "The features say 'Portfolio Mapping' with 'property boundaries, flood zones, and zoning data' -- okay, that's useful for land but I already get that from MLS. 'AI Valuations powered by comps analysis' -- is this a Zestimate competitor? 'Campaign Automation with SMS, email, and direct mail sequences' -- I already have Follow Up Boss for that."

> "This looks like a land investor tool pretending to be for all real estate professionals. Hard pass."

### Assessment

The headline says "real estate professionals" but every signal below it screams land investor / wholesaler. The strategy badges are the tell -- a real estate agent would not recognize themselves in any of those categories. There is no mention of MLS, listings, buyers, sellers, showings, open houses, or brokerage. The visitor correctly identifies this as a niche tool miscategorized with a broad label.

---

## Question 2: VALUE PROP (next 60 seconds)

**Score: 1/5**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "Okay, the features: Portfolio Mapping, AI Valuations, AI Deal Intelligence -- 'Sophie, your AI copilot, scores leads, drafts offers, and surfaces the best opportunities.' That sounds interesting but 'scores leads' means what? Seller leads? I need buyer leads."

> "'Document Generation -- Auto-generate purchase agreements, contracts, and closing documents in seconds.' Hmm, I use DocuSign and my brokerage's forms. Can it generate state-specific forms? Probably not."

> "'Compliance Built-In -- Phone compliance, do-not-call list checks.' Okay, that's useful for cold-calling, but I don't cold-call. My leads come from Zillow, Realtor.com, and referrals."

> "The social proof says '18 Free data sources' and '500+ Properties managed.' Five hundred? That's nothing. My brokerage manages more than that. And 'properties managed' sounds like an investor portfolio, not active listings."

> "This doesn't solve my problem. My problem is managing 30 active clients, coordinating showings, tracking offer deadlines, and keeping my broker happy. None of that is here."

### Assessment

The value proposition is entirely misaligned for this visitor. Every feature is oriented toward acquiring, evaluating, and managing investment properties -- not representing clients in residential transactions. The visitor has zero reason to believe this solves their actual problems.

---

## Question 3: PRICING (2 minutes)

**Score: 3/5**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "Free: $0, 10 leads, 3 properties. Starter: $20/mo, 250 leads, campaigns. Pro: $49/mo, 500 leads, BYOK, unlimited. Scale: $79/mo, 10 seats, unlimited everything."

> "The pricing is clear and reasonable. Annual saves 20%. 14-day free trial on paid plans. I can understand what I'd pay."

> "But '10 leads' and '3 properties' on free -- that's a weird unit. In my world a 'lead' is a potential buyer or seller and a 'property' is a listing. Are they using these words the same way I am? '500 leads' on Pro -- I might have 500 contacts in my CRM but they're not all 'leads.'"

> "What's 'BYOK Data Providers'? Bring your own key? For what? And 'Notes' as a limit -- '2 notes' on free, '25 notes' on Starter. Notes like... text notes? Oh wait, 'seller-financed notes.' That's a financial instrument. Yeah, this is definitely not for me."

> "The feature comparison shows 'SMS/Voice Outreach' only on Pro and Scale. 'Stripe Connect Payments' -- why would a real estate agent need Stripe? We use escrow."

### Assessment

The pricing structure itself is well-presented and transparent. The problem is not the pricing -- it is that the pricing reveals how deeply this product is not for this visitor. The feature comparison table actually accelerates the "this isn't for me" realization.

---

## Question 4: SIGNUP READINESS (5 minutes total)

**Score: 1/5**

### Would you sign up?

**No.**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "I've spent five minutes and I'm confident this is not for me. The landing page says 'real estate professionals' but means 'land investors.' I need a CRM for residential real estate -- Follow Up Boss, kvCORE, LionDesk, even HubSpot. This is none of those."

> "If the headline said 'The operating system for land investors' I would have left in 5 seconds instead of 5 minutes. The broad positioning wasted my time."

### Unanswered questions (moot, but noted)

- Does it integrate with MLS?
- Can it handle buyer/seller transaction workflows?
- Does it support team/brokerage structures?
- Is there e-sign integration?

### Hesitations

The entire product is misaligned. This is not a hesitation -- it is a disqualification.
