# R2-005: Retired Parcel Owner (60s, Tablet, 8 Parcels)

**Persona**: Retired, 63 years old. Owns 8 rural parcels across 3 states (inherited + purchased over decades). Wants to digitally manage them -- track values, taxes, maybe sell a few. Not a "flipper" or "investor" in the active sense. Wants organization.
**Device**: iPad Air, Safari
**Referral**: Google search "land portfolio management software"

---

## 1. CATEGORY (60 seconds)

**Score: 3 / 5**

> "'The AI-Powered Platform for Land Investors.' -- I'm not really an investor. I own land. There's a difference. I'm not buying and flipping. I just need to keep track of what I have."

The headline uses "Land Investors" which implies active deal-making. This visitor is a passive owner. They don't see themselves reflected in the positioning.

> "'Find motivated sellers. Analyze parcels. Send direct mail. Close deals.' -- I don't want to find sellers. I AM a seller, maybe. I don't need to send direct mail. I need to see my 8 properties on a map and know what they're worth."

The subheadline workflow (find sellers -> analyze -> mail -> close) is an acquisition workflow. This visitor has a management/disposition need. Only steps 2 and 4 are relevant to them.

> "How It Works -- step 1 is 'Import or Find Parcels.' Okay, I could import mine. Step 2 is 'AI Analyzes Each Parcel' -- yes, I want valuations. Step 3 is 'Launch Direct Mail Campaigns' -- I don't need that. Step 4 is 'Close Deals & Track Returns' -- maybe."

50% relevance. The platform has features they need but the messaging frames everything around active investing.

> "'Portfolio Mapping -- Visualize every parcel on interactive maps with property boundaries, flood zones, and zoning data.' -- THAT is exactly what I want."

The feature card for Portfolio Mapping speaks directly to this visitor, but it's buried below the fold among 5 other feature cards.

**Comparable**: They don't have a comparable. They currently use a manila folder, a spreadsheet, and county assessor websites. They're looking for "TurboTax but for land management."

**Verdict**: They recognize relevance but don't feel like the primary audience. The "investor" positioning creates distance.

---

## 2. VALUE PROP (60 seconds)

**Score: 3 / 5**

> "'AI Valuations' -- yes. I want to know what my parcels are worth. My kids keep asking and I don't know."

> "'Document Generation -- auto-generate purchase agreements, contracts, and closing documents.' If I decide to sell one of my parcels, this could be useful."

> "'Compliance Built-In -- phone compliance, do-not-call list.' I'm not calling anyone. Why would I need this?"

Half the features are irrelevant (campaign automation, compliance, lead scoring) and half are exactly what they need (mapping, valuations, documents, portfolio tracking). The problem is the landing page doesn't separate "manage what you own" from "find new deals."

> "'18 free data sources' and '$0 to get started' -- I'm retired on a fixed income. Free is important. But '14 day free trial' implies I'll have to pay eventually."

Credibility: No mention of how accurate the AI valuations are. For someone making decisions about estate planning or sales, "powered by 18 open data sources" is not reassuring. They want to know: "Is this as good as a professional appraisal?"

> "Everything here is about running a business. 'Campaign Automation,' 'Close Deals,' 'deal scoring.' I don't have a business. I have land."

**Verdict**: Partial value match. The management features exist but are not foregrounded. The visitor has to mentally filter out 50% of the messaging to find their use case.

---

## 3. PRICING (2 minutes)

**Score: 4 / 5**

> "'Free -- $0 -- 10 leads, 3 properties.' Wait -- 3 properties? I have 8. So Free doesn't work for me."

> "'Starter -- $20/mo -- 50 properties.' That works. But I don't need 250 leads or campaigns. I just need property management."

> "So I'm paying $20/month for features I'll use maybe 20% of? That feels wasteful. But $20 is okay on my retirement budget."

This reveals a pricing/packaging mismatch: a passive owner is buying a Starter plan for 50 property slots but getting leads, campaigns, and sequences they'll never touch. There's no "Owner" tier.

> "The comparison table shows 'Direct Mail Integration,' 'Stripe Connect Payments,' 'SMS/Voice Outreach' -- I don't need any of that. I need maps, valuations, and documents. Those are in the free tier... but only 3 properties."

> "Annual billing at $192/year -- that's $16/month. Okay, that's manageable."

**Verdict**: Price is acceptable but the packaging doesn't fit. They're paying for an acquisition-focused tool to solve a management problem. The free tier's 3-property limit forces them into a paid plan they'll underutilize.

---

## 4. SIGNUP READINESS (5 minutes total)

**Score: 3 / 5**

> "I might try it. But I'm nervous about technology. Is this going to be complicated?"

They tap "Get Started Free" on their iPad.

> "'The operating system for real estate professionals.' -- I'm not a professional. I'm retired. I own some land. Am I in the right place?"

The auth page tagline creates friction AGAIN for a non-professional. Combined with the landing page's "investor" language, this visitor feels like they're entering a tool built for someone else.

> "Sign up with Google -- okay, I have a Gmail. Let me try that."

Clerk SSO works well on iPad. The sign-up flow itself is frictionless.

Hesitations:
1. "Is this going to sell my information?"
2. "Will I be able to figure this out? The landing page mentioned AI and data sources -- sounds complicated."
3. "What if I sign up and it asks me questions I don't know how to answer?"
4. "My grandson could probably help me if I get stuck."

> "There's no phone number for support. No 'Help' link on the landing page. No chat widget. If I get stuck, who do I call?"

Accessibility note: On iPad, the landing page layout works but the nav text is small. The "Get Started Free" button is easy to tap. The pricing cards are a tight 4-column grid that requires horizontal scrolling on iPad portrait mode.

**Verdict**: Tentatively willing to sign up because it's free. But low confidence, no support visibility, and identity mismatch ("professional"/"investor") create real hesitation.

---

## 5. FIRST-RUN MENTAL MODEL (5 min post-signup)

**Score: 2 / 5**

Onboarding wizard path selection: beginner / active / enterprise.

> "'Beginner' -- I've owned land for 30 years. I'm not a beginner at owning land. I'm a beginner at using software. None of these paths describe me."

> "'Active' says 'Upgrade your investing operation.' I don't have an operation."

> "'Enterprise' says 'Configure AcreOS for your team.' I don't have a team."

The path-branching assumes all users are investors at different stages. A passive owner who just wants to catalog their properties doesn't fit any path. They pick "beginner" reluctantly.

> "'Where Do You Want to Invest?' -- I don't want to invest. I want to manage what I own. There's no 'I already own land and just want to track it' option."

The beginner path asks for a target county to INVEST in. This visitor's 8 parcels are spread across 3 states. They don't have a "target county" -- they have 3+ existing counties.

> "'AcreOS Found Real Opportunities' -- I don't want opportunities. I want to add my 8 parcels and see them on a map."

The Instant Deal Hunt (the aha moment for investors) is meaningless and confusing for an owner. They didn't come here to find deals. They came to manage assets.

They eventually reach the Today page. The sidebar:

> "'CRM' -- what's a CRM? 'Skip Tracing' -- what? 'Deal Pipeline' -- I don't have a pipeline. 'Campaigns' -- I'm not running campaigns."

> "Wait -- 'Properties' is in the CRM section. And 'Portfolio Map.' Those are what I need. But they're buried under 'CRM' which is a term I don't know."

> "The getting started checklist says things like 'Add your first lead' and 'Create a campaign.' I don't want to do either of those things. I want to add my first PROPERTY."

**Verdict**: The first-run experience is designed for investors, not owners. Every onboarding step, every nav label, every checklist item assumes acquisition intent. A passive owner is lost by minute 2.

---

## TOTAL SCORE: 15 / 25

**Summary**: AcreOS has features this visitor needs (portfolio mapping, AI valuations, document generation, property tracking) but the entire funnel is framed around active land investing. A retired owner who wants to digitize their portfolio faces: (1) identity mismatch in headline and auth page, (2) onboarding paths that don't include "I already own land," (3) a target county prompt when they need a "add my existing parcels" prompt, (4) a sidebar dominated by acquisition/outreach tools. The free tier's 3-property limit forces them into a $20/mo plan they'll underutilize. This is a real market segment (passive land owners) that AcreOS could capture with minor onboarding changes -- an "I own land and want to manage it" path would solve most of these friction points.
