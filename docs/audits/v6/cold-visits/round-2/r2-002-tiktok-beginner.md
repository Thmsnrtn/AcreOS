# R2-002: TikTok Beginner (Zero RE Experience)

**Persona**: Complete beginner. Saw a TikTok video about "flipping land for profit." Phone only. No real estate experience. 22 years old, works retail, has $3K saved.
**Device**: iPhone 14, mobile browser (Safari)
**Referral**: Link in TikTok bio.

---

## 1. CATEGORY (60 seconds)

**Score: 3 / 5**

> "Okay so it says 'AI-Powered Platform for Land Investors.' I guess I want to BE a land investor? I don't know if I count yet though."

The headline correctly identifies the target audience but this visitor does not yet self-identify as a "land investor." They're curious but not committed. The word "Platform" also reads enterprise -- they were expecting something more like "app" or "tool."

> "'Find motivated sellers. Analyze parcels. Send direct mail. Close deals.' -- I literally don't know what any of that means. What's a parcel? Like a package? What's direct mail, like postcards?"

The subheadline assumes baseline knowledge. "Motivated sellers," "parcels," "direct mail" are industry jargon that means nothing to this person.

> "The How It Works section says 'Pull county data, import CSVs' -- what's a CSV?"

They scroll fast on mobile. The feature cards load nicely but the language barrier is real. They grasp "AI" and "close deals" and "track returns" but the mechanics are opaque.

**Comparable**: They don't have one. They've never used business software. The closest thing they know is Robinhood or Cash App.

**Verdict**: They understand the general category (land investing tool) but cannot map the value proposition to their own situation because they don't know the terminology.

---

## 2. VALUE PROP (60 seconds)

**Score: 2 / 5**

> "'AI Valuations powered by comps analysis and 18 open data sources.' What's a comp? What's an open data source? This sounds smart but I have no idea if I need it."

> "'Sophie, your AI copilot, scores leads, drafts offers, and surfaces the best opportunities.' -- Okay Sophie sounds cool, like an AI assistant. But what's a lead in this context? A person? A piece of land?"

The value proposition assumes the visitor already knows:
1. What land investing IS
2. What steps are involved
3. Why those steps are painful
4. What tools exist to solve them

This visitor knows NONE of those things. They need to be told "Land investing means buying cheap rural land and selling it for more" before any feature makes sense.

> "Campaign Automation -- SMS, email, and direct mail sequences. Am I supposed to TEXT people? That sounds sketch."

> "Compliance Built-In -- phone compliance, do-not-call list. Wait, am I going to be CALLING people? The TikTok didn't say anything about cold calling."

Fear response: The feature list inadvertently reveals parts of the business (cold calling, SMS outreach, compliance issues) that the TikTok glossed over. This creates anxiety rather than excitement.

**Verdict**: The value prop is legit but incomprehensible to someone with zero context. No educational content bridges the gap.

---

## 3. PRICING (2 minutes)

**Score: 4 / 5**

> "'Free -- $0 -- 10 leads, 3 properties.' Okay I can try it for free. That's good because I can't spend much."

> "'Starter $20/mo, Pro $49/mo, Scale $79/mo.' That's pretty cheap? I pay $15/mo for Spotify. $20 is doable if this actually makes me money."

The pricing structure is actually well-suited for this visitor. The free tier lets them explore with zero risk, and the low price points don't feel scary. The 14-day free trial on paid plans adds safety.

> "Feature comparison table is confusing. 'BYOK Data Providers' -- is that a typo? 'Sequences' -- like DNA sequences? 'Stripe Connect Payments' -- I use Stripe to buy stuff online, what does it mean here?"

The table is comprehensive but jargon-heavy. However, the price points themselves are clear and the progression from Free to Scale is intuitive.

> "I don't know what most of these features ARE, but $0 to start means I can just try it and figure it out."

**Verdict**: Free tier is the right answer for this visitor. Price is not a barrier. Comprehension of what they're paying for IS, but the free tier makes that acceptable.

---

## 4. SIGNUP READINESS (5 minutes total)

**Score: 3 / 5**

> "I'll try it. It's free. But like... I don't know what I'm going to DO once I'm in there."

They tap "Get Started Free." The auth page loads on mobile.

> "'The operating system for real estate professionals.' Wait, am I a real estate professional? I work at Target. Maybe this isn't for me."

The auth page tagline is actively discouraging to this visitor. "Professional" implies credentials, experience, licenses. They don't have any of those.

> "Sign up with Google? Okay that's easy."

Clerk SSO reduces friction significantly. They don't have to create a password or verify email manually. This is the save.

Hesitations:
1. "Will this ask for a credit card?"
2. "Is this going to be too complicated for me?"
3. "Will I look stupid if I don't know what things mean?"
4. "Should I learn more about land investing first before signing up for a tool?"

> "There's no 'Learn about land investing' link or 'New to land?' section. It just assumes I already know what I'm doing."

**Verdict**: They sign up because it's free and Google SSO is easy. But their confidence is low and they expect to bounce within 5 minutes.

---

## 5. FIRST-RUN MENTAL MODEL (5 min post-signup)

**Score: 3 / 5**

They hit the onboarding wizard. Path selection: beginner / active / enterprise.

> "'Beginner' -- okay, that's me. 'Welcome to AcreOS -- Let's personalize your experience.'"

> "'Where Do You Want to Invest?' -- invest in what? A county? I don't even know which states have cheap land. Can you just tell me?"

The onboarding asks them to pick a county and state. On a phone, typing a state abbreviation when you don't know which states are good for land investing is a dead end. There's no suggestion engine, no "popular counties" list, no guidance.

> "I typed 'TX' because I've heard of Texas. And 'Travis' because that's where Austin is. Is that a good county for land investing? I have no idea."

The Instant Deal Hunt runs and shows "Found 3 opportunities" with motivation scores, offer prices, resale values. On mobile this is a dense card layout.

> "It says someone named 'John Smith' has 5 acres with a motivation score of 78. Am I supposed to... contact this person? And offer them money? With my $3K?"

The aha moment (real deal data) is powerful for experienced investors but anxiety-inducing for beginners. They don't have enough context to interpret what they're seeing or what to do with it.

They land on the Today page. The sidebar on mobile is a hamburger menu.

> "There's SO MUCH stuff in this menu. CRM, Skip Tracing, Deal Pipeline, Campaigns, AI Hub, Intelligence, Finance... I don't know where to start."

The GettingStartedChecklist provides some direction but the sheer feature surface area is overwhelming on a phone.

> "Nothing on the landing page told me I'd need to know this much stuff. I thought it was going to be simpler."

**Verdict**: The beginner onboarding path exists but doesn't educate. It assumes beginners know the workflow and just need guidance on WHICH county to pick, not WHY they're picking a county or what happens next. The mobile experience compounds the overwhelm.

---

## TOTAL SCORE: 15 / 25

**Summary**: AcreOS is not built for this visitor -- and it probably shouldn't be. The platform correctly targets "Land Investors" in its headline, and this person isn't one yet. However, TikTok-driven traffic is real and growing. The main failures are: (1) no educational content bridge for beginners who don't know industry terminology, (2) the auth page says "real estate professionals" which actively excludes beginners, (3) the onboarding assumes county knowledge that beginners don't have, and (4) the mobile-first experience on the Today page is overwhelming. If AcreOS wants this traffic, they need a "New to land investing?" funnel. If they don't want this traffic, the current filtering is working (barely).
