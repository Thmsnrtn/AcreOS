# R2-001: PropStream Land Investor (3 years experience)

**Persona**: Land investor, 3 years active, uses PropStream for data. Looking for an all-in-one platform to consolidate workflow.
**Device**: Desktop (dual monitor setup)
**Referral**: Heard about AcreOS in a Facebook group for land flippers.

---

## 1. CATEGORY (60 seconds)

**Score: 5 / 5**

> "Okay -- 'The AI-Powered Platform for Land Investors.' That's me. That's literally what I do."

The headline is surgically precise for this visitor. The four-step "How It Works" section maps directly to their existing workflow: import parcels, run comps, send mail, close deals. They recognize every term.

> "Find motivated sellers. Analyze parcels. Send direct mail. Close deals. All in one platform. -- yeah, that's four separate tools I'm paying for right now."

**Comparable**: They immediately benchmark against PropStream + REIPrintMail + a Google Sheet + Podio. AcreOS positions itself as the replacement for all of those.

**Verdict**: Category is crystal clear. Zero ambiguity.

---

## 2. VALUE PROP (60 seconds)

**Score: 4 / 5**

> "AI Valuations powered by 18 open data sources -- interesting. PropStream charges per lookup for some of that. If this actually works, that's a real advantage."

> "Sophie, your AI copilot, scores leads, drafts offers -- I'll believe that when I see it, but if the deal scoring is even close to accurate, that saves me 2 hours per batch."

The "18 free data sources" claim is compelling because they know what paid data costs. However, no specifics on WHICH data sources are used. PropStream names their sources. This visitor wants to know: DataTree? ATTOM? Or scraping county assessor sites?

> "Compliance Built-In -- DNC list checks -- that's actually huge. I almost got fined last year."

Credibility gap: No testimonials, no case studies, no "X users have done Y deals." The social proof section shows "500+ Properties managed" which is modest for a 3-year investor who might personally manage 200+.

**Verdict**: Strong problem-solution match. Credibility is thin but the feature set speaks for itself to someone who knows the space.

---

## 3. PRICING (2 minutes)

**Score: 5 / 5**

> "Free, $20, $49, $79 -- wait, that's it? I'm paying $97 for PropStream alone. Plus $150/month for my CRM. Plus mail costs."

> "Pro at $49 gets me 500 leads, BYOK data providers, unlimited campaigns, SMS/voice outreach -- that's genuinely cheaper than what I'm running right now."

The pricing page is clean. The feature comparison table answers every question: how many leads, properties, AI requests, campaigns, sequences. The "BYOK Data Providers" row on Pro catches their eye -- they already have API keys for some services.

> "14-day free trial on all paid plans. Annual saves 20%. Predictable. I know exactly what I'll pay. Nice."

> "Only concern: 500 leads on Pro. I pull 2,000-lead lists from PropStream. Do I need Scale at $79 for unlimited? Or is 'leads' different from 'list size'?"

Minor ambiguity: "Leads" could mean active CRM records or imported list size. But the pricing structure itself is transparent and competitive.

**Verdict**: Pricing is a legitimate competitive advantage for this persona.

---

## 4. SIGNUP READINESS (5 minutes total)

**Score: 4 / 5**

> "Alright, I'll try it. Free tier lets me kick the tires with 10 leads and 3 properties. Low risk."

They click "Get Started Free" and land on the auth page. Clerk-powered signup with Google SSO option -- smooth.

> "'The operating system for real estate professionals' -- wait, that's different from 'The AI-Powered Platform for Land Investors.' Which is it? Is this for all RE professionals or specifically land?"

The auth page tagline says "real estate professionals" while the landing page says "Land Investors." This creates a micro-hesitation. Not a deal-breaker for this persona, but they notice it.

> "The Adjacent Verticals section on the landing page -- Wholesaling, Fix & Flip, Buy & Hold -- tells me they're expanding. So this is land-first but planning to go broad. That's fine, as long as the land tools stay deep."

Remaining hesitation: No product screenshots, no demo video, no sandbox. They're committing to signup purely on copy and feature lists. For a tool that manages their deal pipeline, they want to SEE the UI before committing time.

**Verdict**: Willing to sign up. One messaging inconsistency noted. Lack of preview content creates friction.

---

## 5. FIRST-RUN MENTAL MODEL (5 min post-signup)

**Score: 4 / 5**

Post-signup, they hit the onboarding wizard. The path-branching (beginner / active / enterprise) is smart -- they pick "active."

> "Import Your Existing Portfolio -- CSV upload, nice. 'Set Your Target Counties' -- this maps to how I actually work. I run deals in 3 counties."

> "The Instant Deal Hunt scanned my target county and found motivated sellers with motivation scores? Okay, NOW I'm paying attention. That's the aha moment."

They land on the Today page. The sidebar navigation is dense but organized:
- CRM group: Leads, Skip Tracing, Properties, Portfolio Map, Deal Pipeline, Marketplace, Listings, Documents, Blind Offer Wizard
- Campaigns: Campaigns, Direct Mail, Sequences
- AI Hub
- Intelligence: Insights, AI Valuations, Land Credit, Markets, Counties, Acq. Radar, Document Intel, Compliance

> "This is a LOT of nav items. There are like 30 links in this sidebar. But the grouping makes sense -- CRM, Campaigns, Intelligence, Finance. I know where to go."

> "Skip Tracing and Direct Mail are right there in the CRM group. That's where I'd expect them. Blind Offer Wizard is a nice touch -- that's a very land-specific tool."

The Today page shows a GettingStartedChecklist which gives them direction. Business Pulse, Pax AI Insights, agent activity -- this feels like a real operating system.

> "It matches the landing page promise. 'All in one platform.' Yeah, it's all here."

**Verdict**: Strong first-run experience. Sidebar is feature-dense but well-organized. The onboarding path for active investors is well-designed.

---

## TOTAL SCORE: 22 / 25

**Summary**: AcreOS is nearly perfectly positioned for this persona. The headline, feature set, pricing, and onboarding all speak directly to an experienced land investor looking to consolidate tools. The main friction points are (1) the "real estate professionals" vs "Land Investors" messaging inconsistency on the auth page, (2) no product screenshots or demo, and (3) the "500+ Properties managed" social proof feeling thin. This visitor signs up on the first visit.
