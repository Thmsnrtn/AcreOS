# Cold Visitor Simulation 010: YouTube Beginner (Phone Only)

**Visitor profile:** Complete beginner. Watched a YouTube video titled "How I Made $30K Flipping Land With No Experience" two days ago. Has $10,000 in savings. No real estate experience, no CRM experience, no business software experience. Uses a smartphone (iPhone) as their primary/only computing device -- does not own a laptop or desktop. Works a 9-to-5 job and would be doing this as a side hustle. Age 27. Found AcreOS by Googling "land flipping software" after the YouTube video mentioned needing a CRM.

**Date:** 2026-04-18
**Pages visited:** Landing, Pricing
**Source URL:** https://acreos.fly.dev/ and https://acreos.fly.dev/pricing (viewed on mobile Safari)

---

## Question 1: CATEGORY (60 seconds on landing page)

**Score: 3/5**

### What this visitor sees

On mobile: the nav bar with "AcreOS" logo, "Pricing," "Sign In," and "Get Started Free" buttons. Below: "Now in Public Beta" badge. The headline: **"The operating system for real estate professionals."** The strategy badges wrap across multiple lines on mobile. Feature cards stack vertically.

### What the visitor says out loud

> "'The operating system for real estate professionals.' Am I a real estate professional? I haven't done a single deal. The YouTube video made it sound like anyone could start. Does this tool require me to already be a professional?"

> "I see 'Land' in those tags. The video talked about land flipping. And 'Wholesaling' -- the video mentioned that too. So this seems related to what I watched."

> "'Replaces your spreadsheets, disconnected tools, and guesswork.' I don't have spreadsheets or tools. I literally have nothing. So I guess I'm starting from zero, which is good? But 'replaces' implies I already have things to replace."

> "'Manages leads, automates outreach, and closes deals -- powered by AI.' I don't have leads. I don't know what outreach means in this context. 'Closes deals' -- I want to close deals! 'Powered by AI' -- cool, AI is everywhere now."

> "'Portfolio Mapping with property boundaries, flood zones, and zoning data.' The YouTube video said I need to look at parcels on a map. This does that? Let me scroll down..."

> "'AI Valuations -- Instant property valuations.' The video said the hardest part is figuring out what land is worth. If this tool can tell me, that solves a big problem."

> "But the page feels like it's talking to someone who already knows what they're doing. I don't know what a CRM is. I don't know what a pipeline is. The video didn't mention any of this."

### Assessment

The visitor finds enough familiar terms from their YouTube education (land, wholesaling, property valuations, maps) to stay engaged, but the page is clearly not written for them. The "real estate professionals" positioning and the assumption of existing workflows create a subtle unwelcome signal. A true beginner-friendly page would say something like "Start your first land deal" or "Everything you need to flip your first property." The current copy assumes the visitor is already operating a business.

I don't know what scoring leads means but 'drafts offers' -- the YouTube video said you need to send offers to landowners. If the AI can write the offer for me, that's amazing because I have no idea how to write a purchase agreement."

> "'18 Free data sources' -- data about what? Properties? Owners? The video said you need to find property owners. If this tool finds them for me..."

> "'$0 to get started.' Good. I have $10K but I need that for my first deal, not for software."

### Assessment

The features that excite this visitor are the exact right ones: AI-generated offers, document generation, and maps. These address the gaps a YouTube education creates -- "I know the strategy but I don't know how to execute the paperwork." The disconnect is that these features are presented alongside CRM concepts (leads, campaigns, sequences, compliance) that mean nothing to a beginner. The page inadvertently tells this visitor "this tool is powerful but maybe not for you yet." A beginner-oriented message like "Start with zero experience" would change the dynamic entirely.

## Question 3: PRICING (2 minutes)

**Score: 4/5**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "Free: $0, 10 leads, 3 properties. Free is perfect. I don't have any leads or properties yet, so 10 and 3 is more than enough to start."

> "Actually, wait -- 10 leads. The YouTube video said you need to contact hundreds of property owners to get one deal. If I can only track 10 leads on free, I'll run out before I even get close to a deal."

> "Starter: $20/mo, 250 leads. That's more realistic. $20/mo is doable -- that's one dinner out. And 250 leads gives me room to actually run a campaign like the YouTube video described."

> "Pro: $49/mo. The YouTube guy specifically said your first deal should profit $5,000-15,000. So $49/mo is like 1% of my first deal profit. That's fine, but only AFTER I make money. Not before."

> "The billing toggle -- Monthly vs Annual. I'll stick with monthly. I'm not committing to a year of something I've never tried."

> "'AI Requests / day' -- 25 on free. I don't know what an AI request is. If I ask Sophie a question, is that one request? 25 per day seems like a lot for someone who doesn't know what to ask yet."

> "The feature comparison table is overwhelming on my phone. I have to scroll sideways to see all four columns. Let me try... okay, I can see that Free has checkmarks for 'Open Data Sources (18),' 'AI Deal Intelligence,' 'Document Generation,' and 'Portfolio Mapping.' So the free plan includes the things I care about most -- maps and document generation. That's actually great."

> "'Team Seats: 1' on free. It's just me, so 1 is fine."

> "I notice there's a 'Contact us' link for enterprise pricing. I'm the opposite of enterprise. I wish there was a 'Just starting out? Here's what we recommend' section."

### Assessment

The free tier is a realistic entry point for this visitor -- the features they care most about (maps, AI, document generation) are included. The 10-lead limit is a friction point that becomes apparent when they apply their YouTube education about needing to contact many owners. The $20/mo Starter tier is affordable and positions well as a "once I'm serious" upgrade. The pricing page is not hostile to beginners, but it's not helpful either -- there is no guidance on which plan a beginner should choose. The mobile experience of the feature comparison table (horizontal scroll) is suboptimal.

## Question 4: SIGNUP READINESS (5 minutes total)

**Score: 3/5**

### Would you sign up?

**Yes, for the free tier. Motivated by excitement from the YouTube video and the $0 entry point. But expects to struggle once inside.**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "It's free. The YouTube video said I need a CRM. This seems like a CRM for land. I'm going to try it."

> "My concerns:"

> "One: I'm on my phone. The website looks okay on mobile but it's clearly designed for a computer screen. The pricing table was hard to read. Will the actual app work on my phone? I don't have a computer at home."

> "Two: I have no idea what I'm going to do once I get inside. The landing page talks about leads, properties, campaigns, AI. Where do I start? Do I find a property first? Or find a lead first? The YouTube video said the first step is to pick a county and pull a list. Can I do that here?"

> "Three: 'Public Beta.' I Googled 'AcreOS reviews' and found nothing. Zero reviews, zero YouTube videos about it, zero Reddit posts. Is anyone actually using this? Or am I the first?"

> "Four: The YouTube video recommended REsimpli and Pebble. I've never heard of AcreOS. Is this legit? The website looks professional but so do scam sites."

> "Five: I don't see a demo video or screenshots. I want to see what it looks like before I put in my email. What if I sign up and it's some complicated dashboard full of graphs and spreadsheets?"

> "Okay, I'll do it. The worst that happens is I waste 30 minutes. 'Get Started Free.' Let me click... it's asking me to sign in with Google or enter my email. I'll use Google."

### Unanswered questions

- Does the app work on a phone screen?
- Where do I start as a complete beginner with no leads or properties?
- Is there a step-by-step guide for first-time land flippers?
- Can I pick a county and find properties/owners to contact?
- How do I send my first offer?
- What does the product actually look like? (No screenshots on landing/pricing)
- Is this company real and trustworthy?
- Can I talk to someone if I get stuck?
- Does the YouTube video strategy actually work with this tool?

### Hesitations

- Zero social proof or external reviews discoverable online
- No screenshots or demo video -- signing up blind
- Phone-only user worried about desktop-designed interface
- "Public Beta" + unknown brand = trust deficit
- No beginner onboarding or educational content promised
- No human support channel visible
- YouTube video recommended other tools (REsimpli, Pebble), not AcreOS

## Question 5: FIRST-RUN MENTAL MODEL (post-signup)

**Score: 1/5**

### What the visitor sees

After signing up via Clerk on mobile, the sidebar becomes a hamburger menu. Tapping it reveals: **Dashboard, Leads, Properties, Deals, Campaigns, Inbox, AI Hub, Intelligence, Finance, Settings.** On mobile, each item takes the full viewport width.

### What the visitor says out loud

> "Okay, I'm in. There's a hamburger menu icon. Let me tap it. Dashboard, Leads, Properties, Deals, Campaigns... okay, a lot of options."

> "I'm on the Dashboard. It's... mostly empty. There are some cards with zeros. '0 leads,' '0 deals,' '0 properties.' No data yet, obviously. But there's nothing telling me what to do next."

> "I remember the YouTube video said: Step 1 is pick a county. Step 2 is pull a list of owners. Step 3 is send letters. Step 4 is get calls back. Step 5 is make offers. How do I do Step 1 here?"

> "Let me try AI Hub. Maybe Sophie can help me. 'Hey Sophie, I'm brand new to land flipping. What should I do first?' Let me see what she says..."

> "If Sophie gives me a step-by-step walkthrough, this could work. But if she gives me a generic response, I'm stuck."

> "Let me try Properties > Maps. The YouTube video showed a guy looking at parcels on a map. There's a map. I can see it. But... how do I find cheap land? Do I search by county? By state? I see a search bar. Let me type 'Arkansas'... okay, it zooms to Arkansas. Now what? I see county outlines but no specific properties. How do I drill down?"

> "Let me try Leads. It's empty. There's an 'Add Lead' button. But I don't have any leads. I need the software to help me FIND leads, not just track them."

> "Campaigns -- the YouTube video said to send mailers. Let me click Campaigns. It says I can create a campaign. But I need a list of people to send to. I don't have a list. Where do I get a list?"

> "I'm going in circles. The tool assumes I already have leads and properties to manage. But I'm starting from zero. The YouTube video made it sound like the CRM would help me find deals. This CRM helps me manage deals I've already found. That's a different thing."

> "On mobile, the interface is functional but cramped. The sidebar menu works. Property detail pages require a lot of scrolling. The map is usable but the parcel boundaries are hard to see on a phone screen. Campaign creation has form fields that are small to tap."

> "I'm going to go back to YouTube and watch more videos about how to actually find land deals. Then maybe I'll come back to this once I have some leads to put in."

### Assessment

The first-run experience fails this visitor completely. The product is a deal management system that assumes the user arrives with leads, properties, and a workflow. A YouTube beginner arrives with nothing but enthusiasm and $10K. There is no onboarding, no "Getting Started" wizard, no educational content, no "Here's how to find your first deal" pathway. The AI Hub (Sophie) is the one potential lifeline -- if Sophie can guide a complete beginner through the process of selecting a county, identifying promising parcels, finding owners, and crafting offers, the tool becomes usable. But that depends entirely on Sophie's capabilities and is not communicated on the landing page or during first-run.

The mobile experience is functional but clearly secondary. The map, which is the most exciting feature for this visitor, loses much of its utility on a 6-inch screen. Campaign creation forms are usable but not optimized for mobile. The hamburger menu works but adds a tap to every navigation action.

## Summary

| Question | Score | Notes |
|----------|-------|-------|
| 1. Category | 3/5 | Finds "Land" badge; some features exciting; "professionals" intimidating |
| 2. Value Prop | 3/5 | AI offers and doc gen excite; CRM jargon creates uncertainty |
| 3. Pricing | 4/5 | Free tier is strong entry; 10-lead limit feels tight per YouTube education |
| 4. Signup Readiness | 3/5 | Signs up because it's free; expects to struggle; no trust signals |
| 5. First-Run | 1/5 | Completely stuck; no leads to manage; no way to find deals; no onboarding |

**Overall: 2.8/5** -- This visitor represents a massive market segment: YouTube-educated beginners who search for "land flipping software" and arrive ready to start. They have money ($10K), motivation (the dream of $30K profits), and zero infrastructure. AcreOS has features that would genuinely help them (maps, AI valuations, document generation, Sophie), but the product assumes the user already knows what they're doing. The disconnect is between "tool that manages your land business" (what AcreOS is) and "tool that teaches you and helps you start your land business" (what this visitor needs).

Key friction points specific to this persona: (1) phone-only usage -- the product works on mobile but is not optimized for it, (2) no lead generation capability -- the visitor cannot go from "I picked a county" to "here are property owners to contact" inside AcreOS, (3) zero onboarding -- no wizard, no tutorial, no "Start here" guide, (4) no educational content bridging YouTube knowledge to platform actions, and (5) no social proof -- the visitor cannot find a single review, video, or Reddit mention to validate the product.

The fix that would have the highest impact for this persona: a first-run onboarding flow that asks "Have you done a land deal before?" and, if the answer is no, walks them through: pick a county, explore the map, find a parcel, run a valuation, generate an offer letter. That single flow would convert this visitor from "confused and leaving" to "excited and engaged."
