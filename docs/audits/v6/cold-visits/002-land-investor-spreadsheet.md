# Cold Visitor Simulation 002: Land Investor (Spreadsheet User)

**Visitor profile:** Active land investor, currently runs business on Google Sheets + county assessor websites. Heard about AcreOS from a Facebook group recommendation. Does 5-10 deals per year, mostly rural vacant land purchased at tax sales and sold via seller financing.

**Date:** 2026-04-18
**Pages visited:** Landing, Pricing, Auth

---

## Question 1: CATEGORY (60 seconds on landing page)

**Score: 4/5**

### What this visitor sees

The headline reads: **"The operating system for real estate professionals"** -- broad, but the strategy badges immediately clarify: **Wholesaling, Fix & Flip, Buy & Hold, STR / Airbnb, Land, Multifamily, Commercial, Creative Finance, Notes.**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "Operating system for real estate professionals... okay, a bit vague. But -- oh, there's 'Land' right there in the badges. And 'Creative Finance' and 'Notes.' That's literally what I do."

> "'Replaces your spreadsheets' -- they're talking directly to me. 'Manages leads, automates outreach, closes deals, powered by AI.' Yeah, I need that. I'm drowning in spreadsheets."

> "The features: Portfolio Mapping with property boundaries and zoning data -- I currently pull that from county GIS one parcel at a time. AI Valuations from 18 data sources -- I spend hours on comps. Document Generation -- I'm using templates in Google Docs. Campaign Automation with SMS, email, direct mail -- I do this manually with stamps and envelopes."

> "This looks like what people in the Facebook group were describing. It's like a REI CRM with built-in tools specifically for land deals."

> "Comparable to... I don't actually know what's comparable. I've heard of Pebble, REISift, maybe InvestorFuse? But nothing specifically for land."

### Assessment

This visitor recognizes themselves quickly. The "Land" badge is the key signal. The feature list maps almost perfectly to their daily pain points. The one weakness: the headline is too broad and could make them wonder if this is really specialized enough for land, or if it's a generic RE tool that happens to list "Land" as one of nine categories.

---

## Question 2: VALUE PROP (next 60 seconds)

**Score: 4/5**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "'Sophie, your AI copilot, scores leads, drafts offers, and surfaces the best opportunities.' That's interesting. I score my own leads by looking at lot size, back taxes, and owner demographics. If the AI can do that automatically, that's a big time saver."

> "'Compliance Built-In -- Phone compliance, do-not-call list checks.' Oh man, I've been worried about DNC compliance. I just skip-trace and call. This would be huge."

> "The social proof: 18 free data sources, $0 to get started, 14-day free trial, 500+ properties managed. Only 500? That's not a lot. Is this thing new? The badge says 'Now in Public Beta.' Okay, so it's early. That makes me a little nervous."

> "But the problem it solves is absolutely my problem. I spend 60% of my time on data entry, mail merges, and manual research. If this actually works, it could save me 20 hours a week."

### Assessment

Strong alignment. The visitor sees their exact pain points reflected in the feature list. The "Public Beta" badge and modest "500+ properties managed" stat create some credibility concerns, but the Facebook group recommendation carries enough trust to keep them moving forward. One gap: there is no testimonial or case study from an actual land investor. The social proof is all abstract numbers, not human stories.

---

## Question 3: PRICING (2 minutes)

**Score: 4/5**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "Free: $0, 10 leads, 3 properties. That's basically a sandbox. Fine for trying it out."

> "Starter: $20/mo, 250 leads, 5 campaigns, 2 sequences. That's probably enough for me right now. I do 5-10 deals a year, so 250 leads is plenty."

> "Pro: $49/mo, 500 leads, SMS/Voice Outreach, BYOK Data Providers. What's BYOK? Bring your own key? So I could plug in my own skip-tracing API? That's actually really useful."

> "Scale: $79/mo, 10 seats. I'm a one-man operation, I don't need that."

> "Annual saves 20%, so Starter would be $16/mo. That's cheaper than my Mailchimp subscription. And Pro at $39/mo is still less than what I pay for skip-tracing alone."

> "The feature comparison table is helpful. Open Data Sources, AI Deal Intelligence, Document Generation, Portfolio Mapping are all included on Free. So the core value is available free. The paid tiers add volume and channels. That makes sense."

> "One thing I don't see: what does 'AI Requests / day' mean exactly? 25 on free, 500 on Starter. If I'm running comps on 50 parcels, does each one cost an AI request? That could be limiting."

### Assessment

Pricing is clear, affordable, and well-tiered for this visitor's scale. The free tier is generous enough to validate the product. The main confusion is around "AI Requests / day" as a metering unit -- it is not clear what counts as a request. The BYOK concept is appealing but unexplained on the pricing page.

## Question 4: SIGNUP READINESS (5 minutes total)

**Score: 4/5**

### Would you sign up?

**Yes, for the free tier.**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "The Facebook group said it was good, the features match what I need, the free tier lets me try without risk, and the pricing is reasonable if I upgrade. I'll sign up and import a few leads to see if it actually works."

> "My one hesitation: it says 'Public Beta.' Am I going to lose my data? Is this thing stable? There's no uptime guarantee or data export mentioned anywhere."

> "Also, I don't see any documentation or help center linked from the landing page. If I get stuck, who do I ask? There's 'Priority Support' on Pro and Scale, but what does free support look like? A Discord? Email? Nothing?"

### Unanswered questions

- Is my data safe in a beta product?
- Can I export my data if I leave?
- What exactly counts as an "AI Request"?
- Does the AI valuation work for rural vacant land, or just suburban residential?
- Can I import my existing spreadsheet?
- Is there a mobile app?

### Hesitations

- "Public Beta" label creates uncertainty
- No visible help/documentation
- No testimonials from real users
- 500+ properties managed is a small number

## Question 5: FIRST-RUN MENTAL MODEL (post-signup)

**Score: 3/5**

### What the visitor sees

The auth page shows AcreOS branding with the tagline "The operating system for real estate professionals" and a Clerk-powered sign-up form. After signing up, they land on `/today` with a sidebar showing: **Today, Pipeline, Money, AI Hub, Settings.**

### What the visitor says out loud

> "Okay, I'm in. 'Today' -- this is like a daily dashboard. Makes sense."

> "'Pipeline' -- leads, deals, and properties hub. So this is where my leads go. That's the CRM part."

> "'Money' -- notes, portfolio, and cash flow. This is for my seller-financed notes. Cool, that's important to me."

> "'AI Hub' -- AI assistant, agents, and automation. This is where Sophie lives, I guess."

> "But wait -- where's the 'Import' button? I have 200 leads in a spreadsheet. How do I get them in? I don't see an obvious import anywhere in the sidebar."

> "And the sidebar only shows 5 items but the landing page mentioned Portfolio Mapping, Document Generation, Campaign Automation, Compliance... where are those? Are they inside Pipeline? Inside Settings? I have to go hunting."

> "Oh wait, there might be more items I can add. Let me look... yeah, the nav is customizable. But how was I supposed to know that? The default view hides most of the features that sold me on the product."

> "Also, the landing page made a big deal about 'Portfolio Mapping' but I don't see a Map item in the default sidebar. I have to discover it myself. That's a disconnect."

### Assessment

The default 5-item sidebar (Today, Pipeline, Money, AI Hub, Settings) is clean but hides the features that motivated signup. A land investor who signed up because of Portfolio Mapping, Campaign Automation, and Document Generation cannot find those features without discovering the nav customization system. The gap between the landing page promise and the first-run experience creates confusion and potential churn.

## Summary

| Question | Score | Notes |
|----------|-------|-------|
| 1. Category | 4/5 | "Land" badge creates quick recognition; headline slightly too broad |
| 2. Value Prop | 4/5 | Strong pain-point alignment; weak social proof (no testimonials) |
| 3. Pricing | 4/5 | Clear, affordable, well-tiered; "AI Requests" unit is confusing |
| 4. Signup Readiness | 4/5 | Would sign up for free tier; "Beta" label and no docs create friction |
| 5. First-Run | 3/5 | Default sidebar hides the features that sold them; no onboarding |

**Overall: 3.8/5** -- This is AcreOS's target customer and the experience is mostly good. The main risks are: (1) the landing page promises features the default sidebar hides, (2) there is no onboarding flow to bridge the gap, and (3) the "Public Beta" label with no social proof from real users creates trust anxiety that only the Facebook recommendation overcomes.
