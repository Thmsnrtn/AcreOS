# The Note That Stopped Paying: What a Note Investor Does the Same Day

A seller-financed note pays on schedule for forty-one months. On month forty-two, the payment does not arrive.

This is the most common workflow event in a note investor's portfolio that is not a routine payment. It is also the workflow most note investors handle worst, because the response is shaped by the first hour after the missed payment is noticed — and most investors notice it three days late, on a Saturday, after the borrower has already moved on to handling another bill.

The platform AcreOS publishes describes this workflow as `tpl_payment_missed_dunning`. It is one of three core note-investor workflows the platform ships ready-to-run, alongside `note_partial_payment` and `note_payoff`. This post describes exactly what fires when the platform detects a missed payment, and what the operator does the same day.

## H2: The trigger: a real event, not a guess

The workflow fires on a specific platform event: `payment.missed`. That event is generated by the note's amortization schedule and the operator's configured grace period. When the scheduled-payment date passes and no payment record has been logged inside the grace window, the event is emitted automatically.

This matters because the response cannot be hand-rolled per note. A note investor with twelve notes can remember which ones are due on the first and which on the fifteenth. A note investor with a hundred and twenty notes cannot. The schedule has to live in the system; the missed-payment detection has to be automatic; the operator's morning queue has to surface the events without the operator having to ask.

## H2: What runs the moment the event fires

The `tpl_payment_missed_dunning` workflow has three actions, and they fire in this order, the same day:

### H3: Action 1 — Notify the team

A warning-level notification posts to the workspace: *Payment missed on Note #[noteId] — [borrowerName]. Amount: $[amount]. Follow up immediately.*

The notification is workspace-scoped, which matters for multi-seat accounts: a note investor running with a virtual assistant or a co-investor sees the same notification surface, with role-gated access to the borrower's contact details. The principal sees everything; the VA sees what the principal has chosen to share.

### H3: Action 2 — Create a follow-up task

A high-priority task is created on the workspace with a one-day due date: *Missed payment — Note #[noteId] ([borrowerName]). Contact borrower to collect overdue payment of $[amount]. Grace period may apply. Check note terms.*

The task includes the note's grace-period field. That is not cosmetic: the response to a missed payment on a note with a 10-day grace period is different from the response to a missed payment on a note with no grace. The task surfaces the term that governs the next decision; the operator does not have to dig.

### H3: Action 3 — Draft the borrower outreach

A first-touch borrower email is drafted automatically: *Hi [borrowerName], We noticed your payment of $[amount] due on [dueDate] has not been received. Please contact us at your earliest convenience to avoid any late fees. Thank you, [orgName].*

The draft is exactly that — a draft. Nothing is sent until the operator reviews and approves. The platform does not auto-send dunning notices, and that is a deliberate design choice, not an oversight. A first-touch dunning email is the first communication a borrower remembers from this note holder for the rest of the relationship; it should not be generic and it should not be wrong. The operator edits, approves, or rewrites.

## H2: What the same-day workflow does not do

Three things the workflow explicitly does not do, and why.

**The workflow does not auto-text the borrower.** Texting is a higher-trust channel and carries different legal exposure under federal and state telecommunications rules. The platform supports SMS as a manual action with explicit per-message consent tracking; it does not fire SMS off a missed-payment workflow.

**The workflow does not contact a third party.** No call to a relative. No mail to a previous address. No employer outreach. These are tactics common in third-party collection that are out of scope for a first-party note holder servicing their own paper; the platform does not enable them and the operator should not run them without counsel.

**The workflow does not begin enforcement.** Foreclosure, repossession, deed-in-lieu negotiation — these are decisions an operator makes with their own attorney, on the operator's timeline. AcreOS tracks the note's enforcement status as a field on the record and shows the calendar; the platform does not pick the date.

## H2: What the operator does in the first hour

The operator sees the missed-payment notification on the morning queue. The next decision is the operator's, and the platform exposes the data the operator needs to make it:

- **The note record.** Original principal, current balance, payment history (which months paid on time, which arrived late, which were missed previously), grace period, interest rate, maturity date.
- **The borrower thread.** Every message between the operator and the borrower, in one chronological view. The platform threads payment receipts, dunning notices, ad-hoc calls logged manually, and payoff inquiries into the same thread.
- **The drafted email.** Editable. Approvable in one click. Cancelable.

The operator decides: send the drafted email as-is, edit it (commonly to soften the tone for a first-time miss, or to harden it for a third miss), call instead of email, or wait — if the borrower has paid late before and the pattern is known, sometimes the right action is no action for forty-eight hours.

The platform does not pick. The platform shows the data and runs the action the operator chose.

## H2: What happens if the payment arrives late

When the missed payment is finally received, a separate workflow — `tpl_note_payment_received_receipt` — fires. The receipt is drafted, the note's payment history is updated, the missed-payment task is automatically closed with a note ("paid late, [N] days past due"), and the audit log records the cycle.

The next month's payment is now tracked against the regular schedule. The previous miss is recorded on the note's history and is visible to the operator and any seat the operator has chosen to share it with. If the same borrower misses two consecutive payments, the platform surfaces the pattern in the borrower-risk view — that is the kind of judgment the platform supports the operator in making, not the kind the platform makes for them.

The AcreOS public landing shows note_investor as a **beta** vertical, and that word is doing honest work. What is built is built: complete vocabulary in the product (a note is called a note, a borrower is called a borrower, a payment is a payment — not a generic "deal"), and named templates for the most common events, including the missed-payment sequence described above. What "beta" marks is that the vertical does not yet close AcreOS's full decision loop — the platform records what happened, but does not yet underwrite and grade its own recommendations for note investing the way it does for a fix-and-flip offer. The hybrid persona — operators who run both land and note books in one workspace — carries the same beta label and the same three note workflows alongside the land-investor templates.

The reason this matters: every other note-investor template the platform ships — partial-payment receipting, payoff drafting, year-end interest summary — runs on the same event-driven pattern. Once the operator has confirmed the platform handles a missed payment honestly, the rest of the note workflow is the same shape. A partial-payment event produces a partial-payment task and a partial-payment receipt. A payoff request produces a payoff-quote calculation and a draft borrower response. The vocabulary is consistent because the work is consistent.

## H2: What the assistant adds

Pax — the AI assistant inside AcreOS — services notes the same way it services parcel leads. Pax drafts the dunning email. Pax surfaces the missed-payment task in the morning queue. Pax cites the data it used: the note record, the payment history, the grace period, the borrower's prior contact history.

Pax does not call the borrower. Pax does not send anything until the operator approves. Pax does not advise the operator on whether to begin enforcement. The Constitution AcreOS operates under says Pax does not give advice that crosses into fiduciary territory; servicing notes is squarely inside that boundary. The operator decides; Pax surfaces the data and offers suggestions.

## H2: State law varies — the operator's counsel decides

A note holder's first-party collection on their own seller-financed note is generally outside the federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act's third-party-collector scope. Some states extend FDCPA-like duties to first parties; some do not. The grace period, late-fee permissions, foreclosure timeline, and required notice content are state-law variables.

AcreOS does not pick the state-law strategy. The platform tracks the fields, runs the workflows the operator configured, and produces the documentation a state-law-aware attorney can act on. The operator's counsel handles enforcement strategy.

## H2: The discipline this builds

A note book that runs on this workflow is a note book that responds to missed payments on day one, not day five. The first-touch borrower email goes out the same day the payment was due. The follow-up task is on the operator's queue before the operator's morning coffee gets cold. The audit log preserves every action the operator took, every action the operator skipped, and the order things happened in.

That is the discipline. The note that stopped paying is going to require attention either way. The question is whether the attention is on the operator's terms — calm, scheduled, documented — or on the borrower's terms — three days late, on a Saturday, after the next bill has already arrived.

The platform's job is to keep the response on the operator's terms. The operator's job is the part that requires judgment.
