**Last updated:** 2026-04-15

## Company Overview

InvestNext is a real estate investment management platform that centralizes capital raising, investor relations, fund administration, and compliance for general partners (GPs) and syndicators. Founded in 2016 by Kevin Heras (CEO), Matthew Attou, and Michael Gisi, the company emerged from the founders' observation that the real estate investment back-office operated as a "black box" full of tedious manual processes.

The platform has grown to serve over 1,600 GP clients managing more than 70,000 global investors across 105,000+ active investments, with $18B+ in transactions processed. InvestNext reports a 96-98% customer retention rate and claims to resolve 95% of support inquiries within 24 hours. The onboarding process is marketed as "the fastest on the market" at 45 days.

## Target Customer

InvestNext's primary customers are:

- **Real estate syndicators** raising capital from passive investors (LPs) for apartment complexes, commercial properties, and multi-family deals
- **Fund managers** running pooled investment vehicles (equity funds, debt funds, REITs, TICs)
- **General partners (GPs)** who need to manage investor relationships, distribute returns, and maintain compliance (506(b)/506(c) offerings)
- **Co-sponsors and JV partners** coordinating capital across multiple parties

The typical InvestNext customer is managing $1M-$500M+ in investor equity across one or more syndications or fund structures. They are institutional or semi-institutional operators, not individual land investors or small-scale flippers.

- Customizable deal rooms for promoting offerings

- Secure ACH inbound funding

## Strengths

1. **Deep distribution/waterfall engine.** The ability to model complex waterfall structures, automatically calculate distributions, and pay investors via ACH in a few clicks is the platform's flagship capability and a genuine differentiator.

2. **Debt fund management.** Unlike most competitors, InvestNext handles debt offerings with configurable terms, maturity dates, and interest rates -- a rare feature in this category.

3. **White-label investor portal.** GPs can present a fully branded experience to their LPs, building credibility without custom development.

4. **Co-sponsor and JV support.** The platform allows multiple co-sponsors to share deal rooms and manage their own investors while maintaining data separation.

5. **Compliance built in.** KYC/AML, accreditation, Blue Sky filings, and Form D support are integrated rather than bolted on, reducing the need for third-party compliance tools.

6. **Fast onboarding and support.** 45-day onboarding and 24-hour support resolution are well-regarded in reviews. Customer retention rates above 96% support this.

7. **SOC 2 Type 2 certified.** Financial-grade security is table stakes for managing investor capital but many competitors lack this certification.

8. **Growing scale.** 1,600+ GP clients and $18B+ in transactions provide meaningful social proof and platform maturity.

---

## Weaknesses

1. **CRM is an afterthought.** Multiple reviewers note the CRM "doesn't work well as a CRM" and requires pairing with HubSpot or another platform. The CRM is investor-relationship focused (pipeline for capital commitments) rather than a general-purpose sales CRM.

2. **No native integrations.** HubSpot, QuickBooks, and other common tools require Zapier. No direct API integrations for major CRM or accounting platforms.

3. **Rigid workflows.** The investor commitment process and other key flows lack adaptability. Users report workflows that don't bend to match their actual processes.

4. **Steep learning curve.** The dashboard is complex and takes time to understand. Document handling and email formatting are described as "clunky."

5. **Email and communications are weak.** Formatting investor updates is difficult, with inconsistent rendering between draft and published views. Users wish the email platform "would be more like MailChimp." No macro-level email open/engagement reporting.

6. **Missing fund consolidation.** Investors who participate in multiple funds cannot see a consolidated view across all their investments in one login.

7. **Expensive entry point.** At $499/mo for the Core tier, InvestNext is prohibitive for small operators or those just starting out. The $99 Fundraising tier is extremely limited.

8. **Limited customization.** Reviewers consistently flag lack of customization options for branding, workflows, and reporting.

9. **No property-level operations.** The platform manages the capital side only. There is no property management, listing management, or operational tooling for the underlying assets.

10. **No land-specific or seller-financing features.** The platform has no tools for direct mail campaigns to sellers, lead scoring based on tax delinquency or absentee ownership, blind offer generation, or note servicing workflows that land investors need.

### Summary

AcreOS and InvestNext serve fundamentally different parts of the real estate value chain with minimal feature overlap:

- **AcreOS** covers the operational side: finding land deals, managing seller/buyer relationships, running marketing campaigns, tracking deal flow, managing seller-financed notes, and operating the business day-to-day.
- **InvestNext** covers the capital side: raising money from passive investors, managing fund structures, distributing returns, and maintaining SEC compliance.

A land investor who buys properties for cash or with their own capital has zero need for InvestNext. A syndicator raising $50M from 200 LPs to buy an apartment complex has zero need for AcreOS. The overlap only exists if a land investor scales to the point of raising outside capital, at which point InvestNext could be a complementary tool rather than a replacement.

## Key Differentiator to Watch

**InvestNext's expansion into CRM and deal origination.** InvestNext already has a basic CRM and has flagged deeper CRM integration (including a native HubSpot connection) as a priority. If InvestNext moves upstream into deal origination and property-level management -- particularly if they target niche verticals like land -- they could become a more direct competitor. The $15M Series B gives them capital to expand.

However, the more likely competitive threat runs the other direction: as AcreOS customers scale and begin raising outside capital, they may need InvestNext-style features (waterfall distributions, LP portals, SEC compliance). AcreOS should monitor whether adding a lightweight "capital raise" module would keep scaling customers in-platform rather than losing them to InvestNext or Juniper Square.

**Bottom line:** InvestNext is not a current competitor to AcreOS. It is a potential future complement or a threat only if AcreOS expands into syndication/fund management, or if InvestNext expands down-market into deal origination for niche asset classes.
