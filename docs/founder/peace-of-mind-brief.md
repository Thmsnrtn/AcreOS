Tom — you asked us to turn the cloud of legal worry into a closed list before you let real customers in. We did. The full checklist has every obligation with an owner; this is the short version: the **five things that actually move risk**, ranked, in plain English. Handle these five and the rest is bookkeeping the team carries for you.

The good news first: AcreOS is structurally a **low-risk launch.** It's software, sold to businesses, with the "we're a tool, you make every decision" line held everywhere it matters, AI disclosed, e-signatures legally sound, and no robo-dialing. The hard compliance work is already in the code. What's left is mostly *paperwork and a few professional sign-offs* — not redesigns.

---

### 1. Form the LLC. (This is the one that actually protects YOU.) — HIGHEST RISK

Right now there's no company — there's just you. Every customer dollar and every "what if they sue" runs straight to your name and your personal assets. The LLC is the wall between *"the business got a complaint"* and *"Tom got sued."* Nothing else on this list matters as much, because nothing else protects you personally.

It's a few hundred dollars and an afternoon (Northwest or Stripe Atlas does formation + registered agent + EIN in one pass). Two things to decide: **(a)** LLC now vs. the C-corp you deferred — I recommend a simple LLC now, and we keep the C-corp shelved exactly as you decided; **(b)** the registered-agent address, which also keeps your Marlborough home address out of every email and the public Terms. One side note: our legal docs currently say *"AcreOS, Inc., a Delaware corporation,"* which isn't true yet — once you pick a form, we fix the docs to match.

**Your move. We can't form it for you, but we've teed up everything around it.**

---

### 2. Have a breach plan + the MA security program ready *before* you need them. — HIGH RISK

If customer data ever leaks, the clock starts immediately — all 50 states have notification laws, and **Massachusetts is one of the strictest** (it even requires a written security program, a "WISP," just for holding a MA resident's data). The mistake isn't having a breach; it's having one with **no plan**. We can write the one-page breach runbook and the WISP draft for you this week — no lawyer required to prepare them. This is also the single best argument for cyber insurance (#4): those policies come with a lawyer on call the moment something happens.

**We prepare it; you just need to be okay with us spending the time.**

---

### 3. Get two professional sign-offs — a lawyer hour and an accountant hour. — MEDIUM-HIGH RISK

This is the honest line we won't blur: the team drafted your Terms, Privacy Policy, and Data Processing Agreement, and they're genuinely strong — but **we are not your lawyer.** One 2-3 hour pass by a business attorney (~$150-300/hr) turns "well-written" into "enforceable as intended," especially the liability cap and the class-action waiver. Separately, **one accountant hour** on sales tax: SaaS is taxable in a chunk of states (MA included), and while you're far below every threshold today, it grows silently with revenue. You don't owe anything now — you just want to know *when* the meter starts.

**Your call on timing and budget.** If money's tight, you can launch on the strong drafts and make the lawyer review a fast-follow — but the entity (#1) should not wait.

---

### 4. Carry E&O + cyber insurance. — MEDIUM RISK

Insurance is the backstop for the two things you can't fully engineer away: a customer claiming our data or Pax "cost them a deal" (that's **Errors & Omissions**), and a data breach (that's **cyber**). For an early SaaS this is often a bundle in the low four figures a year. The "tool not advisor" posture makes a claim *less likely*; insurance covers the cost of *defending one anyway*. A broker (Vouch, Embroker) prices it — we can't, and we won't pretend to.

**Your decision + a broker's quote.**

---

These live on surfaces other team members own (Iris, Krieger, Soren) — so this is me chasing down confirmations, not you doing anything. I'll close these and report back.

**The team handles this; I'll bring you a clean "all verified."**
