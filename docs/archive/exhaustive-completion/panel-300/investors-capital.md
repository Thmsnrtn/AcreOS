## 136. Ashok Bhatt — Series-A Lead
**Lens:** TAM defensibility and multi-vertical thesis.
**Backstory:** Vertical-SaaS thesis; returned from 20-panel; obsessed with TAM defensibility at every vertical.
**What I see:** The D1 decision (raise vs exit vs bootstrap) hinges on whether the multi-vertical narrative is credible. Ashok's thesis: "Vertical-packs as the moat" — breadth IS the Series-A story. But there are zero paying customers today (`post-may1-resweep.md` reveals "no paying customer on any vertical yet"). TAM defensibility means: how much revenue from Land alone is enough to justify 90 days on Notes before switching?
**Highest-leverage move:** Model two TAM scenarios: (a) single-vertical (Land only) at $500M addressable, (b) multi-vertical (Land + Notes + BH + etc.) at $2B+ addressable. Show which scenario justifies a $60M post-Series-A valuation. Then present to Asher + board: "If we pick Land-only (D2 path), Series-A is hard. If we pick multi-vertical (D2 path), Series-A is easier." Let the TAM analysis drive the D2 decision, not the other way around.
**Biggest risk:** If you raise a Series-A on a multi-vertical pitch, then pivot to Land-only 6 months in, the board will view it as a bait-and-switch.

## 137. Lucette Marchand — Growth-stage VC

**Backstory:** Series-B/C check-writer; obsessed with magic-number math (NRR ÷ CAC payback period).

**Highest-leverage move:** Model the expansion math: (a) assume 50 Land customers at 90 days, (b) assume 10 NI customers at 90 days, (c) assume Land blended ACV = $3K, NI blended ACV = $10K. Then at 180 days, model churn (assume 5%), expansion (assume 1.2x cohort NRR), new customers (assume 2x monthly growth). Show the path from $100K ARR (today) → $500K ARR (180d) → $1.5M ARR (365d). Lucette wants to see that math before she writes a check.
**Biggest risk:** If the magic-number math doesn't work (e.g., $500K ACV Land customers but $3K SMB customers are your actual TAM), the Series-B will be off-limit.
