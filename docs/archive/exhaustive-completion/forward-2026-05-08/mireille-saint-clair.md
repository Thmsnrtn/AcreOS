**State read (1 paragraph):**
AcreOS has zero growth loops today. Camila's CS automation (pre-churn ladder, power-user identification, NPS capture) is retention machinery — it's excellent, it's necessary, but it's not a loop. A loop is when a customer action creates a side effect that brings in a new customer. Notion's loop: create a shareable page → your collaborator sees Notion → they sign up. Linear's loop: invite your co-founder → both of you adopt → you invite the team. Loom's loop: send a recorded video → the recipient watches → they sign up to record. Deal-Rooms is AcreOS's closest-to-viral surface: you create a deal-room link, share it with a seller or co-investor, they click, they see the data, they sign up. But today deal-rooms don't auto-create signup funnels; they're just data-share links. The invitation doesn't say "join us on AcreOS." The deal-room view doesn't have a "I want this for my own deals" CTA. The loop is 80% built; the last 20% (the viral vector) is missing. *That* is the move. Not "build a referral program" (Camila's approach). Not "ship a community feature" (classic misdirection). Ship the loop that's 80% done, measure it weekly, and let the product do the growth work.

**Push forward — my 5 moves (ranked):**

1. **Retrofit deal-rooms to be the growth loop — add a "join AcreOS" CTA and an unauthenticated signup path** — today, a shared deal-room link shows data but requires authentication. Change it: unauthenticated view shows the deal structure + comps + Pax's top-3 recommendations (a hook). At the bottom: "Want to build deals like this? AcreOS is free for 14 days." Click → signup → they land in AcreOS with their deal already loaded. This is a 3d build: render deal-room data unauthenticated, add signup CTA, auto-populate their org's first deal from the link. The metric: "deal-room shares that converted to signup" — measure this weekly. If it's ≥5%, you have a loop; invest in it. If it's <2%, the loop isn't ready and you revert to paid. — *Why now: the deal-room feature is live; waiting 6 months to retrofit it is leaving compounding growth on the table.*

2. **Measure the loop weekly and set a reactivation metric before launching paid acquisition** — the loop metric is: deals shared → new signups → aha moment (first scan completed) → retention (D7 return). Plot this as a waterfall weekly. If the funnel is 5% → 3% → 2% (i.e., you're losing 60% at every step), the loop is broken and you need to fix the product, not increase share volume. This is the hard discipline: don't ship paid acquisition until the organic loop converts at least 3% of deal-room viewers. Once the loop is 3%+, paid acquisition becomes a leverage lever (you spend $10 to acquire a customer who brings $25 of lifetime loop-value). — *Why now: pre-launch, you're bootstrapped on founder credibility and operator networks (Wendell's friends = free distribution). Once you launch, the paid-acquisition pressure will be enormous; a healthy loop prevents the "we spent ourselves into insolvency" trap.*

3. **Build the "invite your co-investor" network expansion on top of the loop** — Notion's magic wasn't just "share a page"; it was "collaborate with 5 people simultaneously." AcreOS's analog: "invite your co-investor or co-flipper to review this deal." Add a one-click invite that opens a deal-room share link + a warm email template ("Hey, I'm using AcreOS to track land deals. Check out this one I'm considering. Thoughts?"). If the co-investor clicks, they see the deal-room, then the signup CTA. This is the network-effect edge: each deal-room creates leverage for the primary user to bring in secondary users. 2d build, enormous upside. — *Why now: Wendell probably knows 10 other land investors; if each deal you create gets co-investor feedback, 20% of those co-investors eventually sign up for their own deals. That's a 2x loop at the operator level.*

4. **Defer referral-program formality (points, discounts, swag) until the organic loop hits 10% conversion** — referral programs sound good in pitch decks and deliver nothing at $100K ARR because the baseline organic conversion is too low to amplify. Right now, Camila's recommendation is to build a referral program to drive word-of-mouth. Instead: measure the natural deal-room loop. Once it's 8–10% conversion (meaning 10 deals shared → 8–10 organic signups), then a referral program (reward Wendell $100/mo for each land investor he brings) becomes a 2x lever on an already-healthy base. Without the base, it's spending to acquire growth-loop customers who would have signed up anyway. — *Why now: we're weeks away from launch; a half-built referral program is a distraction from fixing the loop we have.*

5. **Wire deal-room data into the customer interview loop so you can reverse-engineer the "why" behind viral deals** — Camila's framework has founder doing 3 calls/week with customers (good). Add one more call: "What's the deal you'd want to share with a co-investor? Why?" Record the answer. Cluster the patterns. If 80% of deal-shares are "comps are the thing I want to validate," your deal-room loop should lead with comps, not structure. If 80% are "I want a second opinion on price," the loop should emphasize Pax's valuation recommendation. This is the feedback-loop that turns a generic viral feature into a targeted one. 1d build, massive insight per week. — *Why now: the loop lives or dies on the question "why would you share this with someone else?" That question has one answer per customer; you have to ask it.*

**What I'd defer (and why):**
- Community features (forums, Slack group, customer advisory board) until 50+ customers (network effects need critical mass; building community at 5 customers is theater)
- Marketplace for deal syndication until the product is 6 months in the field (you're not ready to be a middleman in deals; you're still learning how operators actually use the software)
- API + integration partnerships until you've hit 2K customers (integrations multiply complexity and lower CAC at scale; at 100 customers they're a distraction)

**What scares me most:**
Camila and I are going to have a real fight about loops vs CSM ops, and I'm concerned the founder will hear "customer success is the moat" and defund growth-loop work because it feels riskier. The truth: both are real. But the sequencing matters. You can't CSM your way to $30M ARR if your acquisition CAC is $3K and your ACV is $5K. You *can* if you have a 5% organic loop because every paying customer brings 1 co-investor, who brings 1 deal, who brings 1 friend. Camila's pre-churn ladder + power-user identification is the retention gear. The deal-room loop is the acquisition gear. They're not either/or — they're both. But if I had to choose, the loop is the one that doesn't break at scale. A 50-person CS team can't retain your way to $100M ARR anymore; a 5% daily loop can. That's the move AcreOS should bet on.
