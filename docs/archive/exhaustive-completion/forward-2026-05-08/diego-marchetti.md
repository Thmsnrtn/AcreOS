**Push forward — my 5 moves (ranked):**

1. **Ship founder-letter infrastructure and commit to weekly + archive.** Asher's memo signals narrative urgency; the founder is the narrative. Build: (a) new route `/founder/letter` (archive of past 12 weeks), (b) email template (500-word form, subject line, call-to-action), (c) weekly send to all users + public landing page (site/founder-letter), (d) Git-backed storage so founder's memos become CLAUDE.md precedent. Why weekly? Notion Ambassadors did daily; Linear does weekly; weekly is the right cadence for SMB founder time. The first 12 weeks: founder writes about the last week's work (shipped features, customer wins, what broke, what's next). This isn't a "monthly state of the union"; it's "CEO told you what they worked on this week." Archives become SEO gold (founder candor is rare in proptech). — *1w for infrastructure; ongoing 1hr/week for founder*

2. **Build deal-room shareability: customers export solved deals to a community feed.** You have a deals table. A customer marks a deal as "closed" or "in-progress." Add a checkbox: "share this deal-story in the community?" (default off). If checked, a sanitized version (no PII except deal size + property type + outcome) gets posted to `/community/deal-stories`. Other customers see it, learn ("oh, this investor structured it as a Delaware LLC, I should try that"), and recruit from it. The feed becomes social proof at scale. One customer's solved problem is the next customer's validation. Infrastructure: (a) `community_deal_posts` table (user_id, property_id, deal_summary sanitized, posted_at), (b) export button on `/deal/:id` ("Share this story"), (c) moderation queue (founder approval before posting, 24-hour SLA), (d) lightweight social (other users can "react" or comment). Low lift; high signaling value. — *2w for infrastructure + moderation setup*

3. **Host a weekly live call: "Founder office hours + deal showcase."** 30 minutes, Thursday 10am PT (time zone that works for California land investors). Attendees: 2-3 customers, the founder, maybe a guest (lawyer, contractor, lender). Format: first 10 min (founder hot takes + shipped this week), next 15 min (customer showcases their deal-story for 3 min, community Q&A), last 5 min (next week's topic + sign-off). Recording goes in `/community/calls` (unlisted on YouTube; linked from founder-letter archive). Why Thursdays? Weekly cadence is what sticks (same time = higher attendance). Why this format? It's not "product demo" (boring) or "TED talk" (too formal). It's "founder + peer learning." The invite is to "customers who've been active on deal-room in the last 30 days," so there's a small-group intimacy (not all-hands). This is where community members realize they're part of a movement, not just using software. — *setup 1d; ongoing 30min/week founder + 30min/week moderation*

4. **Recruit 5-10 community ambassadors in Q2, operationalize in Q3.** Ambassadors are the customers who write the most deal-stories, ask thoughtful questions in calls, answer peer questions. Approach them: "You're one of our best operators. Will you help by answering questions in the community?" No pay; the ask is "1-2 hours/week." The upside for them: visibility (listed as "Community Guide" on their profile), early access to features, quarterly summit in Austin (founder pays for travel if AcreOS revenue allows). This is how founder-led community scales (founder can't answer every question; ambassadors multiply the effect). Notion had 50K ambassadors; you'll have 5-10. That's enough to keep the 200-customer community warm. — *Q2 recruitment; Q3 onboarding + formalization*

5. **Commit to "community-first" customer education.** Instead of "Sales call → onboarding call → training docs," try: "Sales call → live community call (customer attends, sees peer use cases) → founder letter + deal-room (self-serve) → escalation to onboarding." This inverts the CAC: you use community for low-touch customer education, onboarding focuses on high-touch only (stalled deals, edge cases). The payoff: onboarding cost drops 20-30%, ramp time is tighter (peer pressure + visible proof of use). Operationalize: add `/community/learning` with transcripts of past calls, curated deal-stories by vertical, weekly founder letter clips (5-min video). — *1w for curation; ongoing 2hr/week for content prep*

**What I'd defer (and why):**
- Paid Slack or Discord. Notion tried this (Notion Slack workspace for power users); it gated the community. The free Discord for Linear has 40K members and feels like spam. Keep community free, async-first, lightweight. If you need real-time chat, you've already lost (founder time is the scarce resource, not chat speed).
- "Community app" — branded mobile app, custom UGC feed, leaderboards. Too early. You have 200 customers; the infrastructure ROI doesn't exist. Focus on founder letter + deal-stories + weekly calls. That's 6 weeks. If you hit 500 customers and community CAC is 3x lower than other channels, then revisit app.

**What scares me most (one named risk + mitigation):**
*Founder burnout from weekly cadence.* Notion's ambassador program worked because Notion had 200+ employees; founder (Ivan) showed up but the program had operational depth. AcreOS is smaller. If the founder commits to weekly letters + weekly calls and then stops after 8 weeks (unavoidable: fundraising, product crisis, travel), the community feels abandoned and churn accelerates. Mitigation: (a) build infrastructure first (letters are stored in Git, calls recorded + auto-posted), so continuity survives a brief pause; (b) recruit ambassadors early so founder isn't the only voice; (c) set explicit expectations ("founder letter = Thursday 5pm PST deadline; call = Thursday 10am PT; I commit for Q2/Q3; we revisit in Sept"). Transparency beats silence.
