Tried to localize a US-built proptech to UK in 2020; hit a wall on Land Registry API quirks, stamp duty per-postcode variance, and FCA rental-insurance rules. The ones that port: lead workflow, deal pipeline, comp tools (with local data).

**Push forward — my 5 moves (ranked):**

1. **Audit which features are US-specific vs portable before vertical #7.** You have 6 verticals; none are international. Before you add #7 (Wholesale Lending or otherwise), run an audit: (a) mark every feature with `regions_available` enum (US/UK/AU/CA), (b) highlight the US-only surfaces (tax-delinquent, 1099s, contract-for-deed, 50-state late-fee rules), (c) identify the portable ones (lead-source integrations, deal-pipeline tracking, property-comp data sources, financing calculators). Results: maybe 40% of AcreOS is portable; 60% is US-specific. That ratio is your beachhead signal. — *1d; information only*

2. **Plan a UK property-comp dataset integration as a pilot.** The comp-spot surface (AI-driven comp analysis) is portable but useless without UK comps. UK's Land Registry publishes sold-price data (free public API); Rightmove has rental comps (paid API, widely used by UK investors). Implement: new `property_data_sources` table with `region`, `datasource_id`, `api_endpoint`, `auth_required`; wire Comp-Spot's backend to query the right datasource based on property.region (which would default to UK for a UK tenant). 3 weeks, not 3 days — Land Registry API is quirky, and you need to handle postcode-to-geom conversion. But this is the proof-of-concept that AcreOS's core analysis engine ports. — *3w; pilot for international scalability*

3. **Don't launch international until you've stabilized 2 US verticals at $500K ARR each.** Phoebe disagrees with Caspar on sequencing here: Caspar says "one US vertical to $1M ARR, then consider #7." Phoebe says "UK is more attractive than Wholesale Lending, and the window is now (2026-2027 before UK-native proptech consolidates)." But the compromise: don't touch UK until you've proved repeatable unit economics on *two* US verticals. Why? Because the international playbook is harder than US expansion (you need local data partnerships, regulatory counsel, currency handling, entity formation). If your US verticals aren't sticky, international will kill you. Gate: two US verticals at $500K ARR each, then greenlight a UK pilot office + hire a UK-based PM. — *deferral + disagreement with Caspar*

4. **For international beachhead, focus on Buy-To-Let landlords, not first-time buyers.** The US land-investor persona (Wendell) is a professional with 12 years experience, $5M+ portfolio, 50+ properties, sourcing via tax auctions + off-market deals. UK's analog isn't "first-time homebuyer"; it's the "Buy-To-Let landlord" — similar operator type, same cash-flow obsession, same "can I build wealth via real estate" motivation. The good news: AcreOS's portfolio-tracking, cash-flow modeling, and deal-sourcing workflows map directly to BTL use cases. The risky news: BTL landlords use different leverage products (buy-to-let mortgages, which have stricter affordability rules than US conforming mortgages). The sourcing is also different (UK doesn't have tax-delinquent auctions; properties come via estate agents and off-market pocket deals). Phoebe's recommendation: build for BTL landlords, not residential first-time buyers. The overlap with your US persona is highest. — *strategy, not effort*

5. **Defer Australia and Canada until UK is at $1M ARR.** Phoebe built in Sydney and knows the AU market. It's tempting — English-speaking, similar investing culture, proximity to AcreOS founder networks. But AU is 1/8th the scale of UK, and the property-data game is even more fragmented (no Land Registry equivalent; Realestate.com.au is the closest). Canada is similar (smaller market, Toronto dominates). UK should be the beachhead; once UK is $1M+ ARR, *then* add AU/CA as #2 and #3 international. — *deferral*

**What I'd defer (and why):**
- Building for EU (France, Germany, Benelux). The regulatory fragmentation is too high (6 different property-registration systems, 6 different tax regimes, 6 different underwriting standards). UK is a single system (Land Registry, single tax code via HMRC, standardized mortgages). Same with Asia-Pacific — too early. Focus on UK first, then AU/CA, then maybe reconsider EU in 3 years.

**What scares me most (one named risk + mitigation):**
*Regulatory landmine on landlord screening.* BH's tenant-screening product (RS-1/RS-2) is built for US FCRA rules. The UK has equivalent regulation (Data Protection Act 2018, Tenant Fees Act). But UK's rules are *stricter* — you need explicit agency agreement and the agency is liable for discrimination lawsuits, not the tenant. If AcreOS enters UK without rebuilding tenant-screening for UK law, you'd face class-action exposure equivalent to the US FCRA space. Mitigation: the moment UK BTL expansion is on the roadmap, commission a UK employment/property lawyer to audit BH against UK tenant law. Budget 3-4 weeks external counsel. Don't port BH to UK before that audit is done.
