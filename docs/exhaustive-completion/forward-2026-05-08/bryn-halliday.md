AcreOS has shipped the rock-solid path: unified pricing, 21/24 P0s, 5/6 verticals customer-launchable, zero technical debt that breaks a Series-A pitch. The founder has the operator credibility (200 deals, landed Thomas Norton) and the voice (the landing letter is the strongest competitive position in the market). The investor's lens: if you're a 2-person vertical SaaS at $500K ARR with 4 planned verticals, you look like either (a) a vertical-SaaS leader compounding to $10M+ ARR (7–9x multiple at exit, 2-3 years, worth $70–90M), or (b) a SMB-ops platform trying to be a horizontal play (flattens to 4–6x multiple, takes 4+ years, exits at $30M). The narrative you ship in month-1 of customer acquisition determines which path the market believes. That narrative should be explicit now, not evolved through hindsight.

**Push forward — my 5 moves (ranked):**

1. **Decide the Series-A narrative lock (vertical-SaaS leader vs SMB-ops platform) and write the founder-memo that defends it** — Tegan will push for per-seat pricing (looks like AppFolio → 10x multiple). Ana will push for masthead-brand-with-verticals (looks like Stripe → growth story, 14x multiple). Bryn's job is to name what each narrative earns. Vertical-SaaS narrative: "We own Land investing software; we own Notes; we own Wholesaling. Highest-multiple exit, 2-3 year runway." SMB-ops narrative: "We're building the 'Stripe for real estate'; we own the entire deal lifecycle; subscription is the floor, verticals are the ceiling." Procore's narrative was SMB-ops (all verticals, one software); it traded at premium. But Procore took $700M in funding and 8 years to exit at $10B. AcreOS doesn't have that runway. Pick one and lock it in copy, pricing, and roadmap. — *Why now: your first 5 customers define whether the Series-A investor believes you're vertical-focused or horizontal-expanding.*

2. **Right-size the Series-A target and backsolve the 18-month KPIs** — if vertical-SaaS narrative, target ~$3–5M ARR, 50–100 customers, 40–50% net retention. If SMB-ops narrative, target ~$2–3M ARR, 300+ customers, 30% net retention. These are the milestones that justify a 7–9x or a 4–6x multiple respectively. Work backward from month-18: what has to be true on Day 1? How many inbound leads? What conversion rate? What CAC payback? What churn? The investor thesis dies in the details; lock them now. — *Why now: the next 6 months of hiring, spending, and roadmap should ladder toward explicit Series-A metrics, not hope.*

3. **Commit to per-seat pricing as the public-market anchor** — Bryn will argue: AppFolio ($1.6B market cap, 10x ARR) is the comp. Procore ($30B, 14x ARR) is the stretch. Both use per-seat pricing because it scales predictably and it's what public-market models demand. Tegan's flat-seat-ceiling model is ROI-optimal for customers; it's not what a Series-A investor models. Compromise: ship flat pricing now (customer retention), but wire the per-seat metering in the backend (`SeatUsageThresholds` table exists, see `autonomousSalesPipeline.ts`), so the Series-A round can toggle to per-seat pricing for new customers post-funding without a product rebuild. — *Why now: starting the conversation with investors at "we price flat" is starting with a credibility tax. Starting at "we've built per-seat metering; we chose not to activate it yet" is a founder-voice move.*

4. **Define the "when to IPO" watermark and the exit-floor valuation** — realistic: AcreOS won't IPO alone. The exit scenario is either (a) acquihire by a platform (Zillow, Redfin, REI Pro parent) at $50–150M, or (b) Series B by a platform investor (Salesforce Ventures, Sapphire) followed by acquisition of the platform by a larger platform. The timeline: Series A in month 6–9 (when you've proven $200K+ MRR and 50+ customers). Series B in month 18–24 (when you've hit $500K+ MRR and multi-vertical traction). Exit scenario (acq or secondary) by month 30–36 (when you've hit $1M+ ARR or proven the platform model at scale). If that feels too short, you've priced too low or overestimated TAM. — *Why now: the founder's personal financial planning (equity, dilution expectations) should ladder toward realistic exits, not the "$100M valuation" dream.*

5. **Benchmark against three public comps, quarterly, and present the "multiple trajectory" to the board every month** — AppFolio = 10x (software, all-in-one, horizontal). Procore = 14x (SMB-ops, vertical adjacency, horizontalizing). ServiceTitan = 12x (SMB, high net retention, multiple verticals). AcreOS at $500K ARR today is pre-revenue for any of these models. But by month-12 (target $1.5M ARR), you want to see a pattern: "AcreOS is trading at Procore-equivalent (12x) if we hit 50% net retention; at AppFolio-equivalent (10x) if we hit 25%." This is the metric the Series-A investor will use to model exit value. If you're below Procore-equivalent by month-12, the Series-A narrative resets downward. — *Why now: board discipline on public-market comp tracking is how founders avoid the AppHarvest trap (narrative inflation until the market calls it).*

**What I'd defer (and why):**
- IPO readiness (audit, SOC 2, investor relations) until Series A closes (it's month-12+ work; it's not on the path to Series A; put it in the Series B prep list)
- M&A traction (acquisition of complementary startups) until $2M+ ARR (you can't integrate acquired tech at $500K ARR; you're just adding debt)
- International expansion (UK/AU land-investing) until domestic (Land + Notes + Wholesaling) hit product-market fit at scale (it's a $500K+ year-2 workstream; it's not Series-A traction)

**What scares me most:**
The moment that breaks startups is when founders believe the VC narrative instead of the unit economics. AppHarvest built beautiful greenhouses and lost sight of the fact that the software model never existed. AcreOS has ship-discipline and operator credibility, which is rare. The risk is that Bryn (the investor perspective) and Tegan (the pricing strategist) will have a real fight about per-seat vs flat, and the founder will split the difference in a way that confuses both customers and investors. AcreOS needs to pick one narrative — vertical-SaaS leader or SMB-ops platform — and commit to it in pricing, roadmap, and comp comms. That commitment is the Series-A credibility move. Without it, the investor reads it as "we haven't decided what we are," which kills a 7–9x multiple down to a 4–6x immediately.
