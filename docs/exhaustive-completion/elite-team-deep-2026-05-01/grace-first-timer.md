# Grace Peterson — first-timer review

I'm 32. Boise. I write Java for a logistics company by day. Last summer I bought a 2.4-acre lot outside Mountain Home for $14,000 cash because a guy on the BiggerPockets podcast made it sound easy and my brain wouldn't let it go. I haven't done anything with it yet. The deed is in a manila folder in my filing cabinet and I'm pretty sure I owe $89 in property tax that I should probably have already paid.

I want to do this for real. Five to ten deals a year, on the side, building toward something. I've been reading Mark and Tate and Seth's blog posts and I have a Google Doc with 41 saved listings off LandWatch. I have a column in my spreadsheet called "APN??" because I keep seeing the term and I'm still not 100% sure what it means versus a parcel number versus a tax ID. (I think they're the same thing? But I'm not betting $14K on "I think.")

Wendell looked at AcreOS like a mechanic looks at a car. I'm looking at it like someone who just bought their first toolbox and is afraid to open it because she might use the wrong wrench and break something expensive.

Here is my honest first-timer reaction.

---

## 1. Thirty-second verdict

**Would I sign up?** Probably not today. Maybe the Free tier just to look around, but I'd close the tab inside a week.

Not because the product is bad. Because the product is not for me. It's for Wendell. Every word, every menu, every "operating system for Land Investors" tagline tells me I'm crashing somebody else's party. The landing copy says "for solo investors, partners, and small teams" — which sounds inclusive until you read on and the next sentence is "three AI agents do the busy work — comping, replying, servicing notes." I had to look up "comping" (comparable sales, fine) and "servicing notes" (collecting payments on owner-financed deals — I won't be servicing a note for at least three years because I'm not selling on terms yet, I just want to buy one rural lot and resell it for cash on Facebook Marketplace).

**Would I sign up at $20 Starter?** Maybe. I spend $14 on Spotify and $11 on Hulu and I once paid $99 for a course that was just a guy reading his book at me, so $20 is not the issue. The issue is whether I'd cancel after month one because I logged in twice and got overwhelmed both times.

**Would I sign up at $79 Scale?** No. That's $948 a year. I made $3,400 net on my one deal last year (still mid-flip, may lose money). I am not paying 28% of my hypothetical deal profit to software. Wendell did the same math from the other end — for him $79 is too cheap-feeling for the volume he wants. For me it's a third of a deal.

**The honest answer:** I'd sign up for the Free tier, log in twice, get scared, and go back to my spreadsheet. Which is the worst possible outcome, because that's a signed-up user the team thinks they have who's actually already gone.

## 2. The first 5 minutes

I land on `/auth?mode=register`. I sign up with my Gmail. I get dropped into onboarding-v2.

The first screen asks me to pick a path: **beginner / active / enterprise**. Okay, I'm beginner. I click it. (Already a small problem: I almost clicked "active" because I have ONE property and I am, technically, actively trying to sell it. The labels lean toward your existing self-image more than your actual situation. "Active" sounds like the grown-up answer. I clicked it the first time, panicked when it asked me to import my portfolio, hit back, picked beginner, and felt embarrassed. Nobody saw it. But I felt it.)

Beginner path is six steps:

1. Welcome
2. Pick a target county
3. Instant deal hunt
4. Strategy
5. Pax tour
6. Done

**Step 2 — pick a county.** I don't have a target county. I have "somewhere within a 4-hour drive of Boise where I can afford to buy something cash." I want a screen that asks me my budget, my drive radius, and what I want out of land (flip fast / hold and sell on terms / build a cabin / I don't know yet) and then suggests three counties. Instead I'm staring at a county picker like I'm supposed to already know. I'd type "Elmore County, ID" because that's where my one lot is. Fine. Moving on. But if I were truly green I'd pick the county next to me at random and the rest of the onboarding would be subtly wrong.

**Step 3 — Instant Deal Hunt.** This is supposed to be the magic moment. AcreOS shows me real parcels in Elmore with motivation signals. Wendell will use this to pressure-test the data quality. I'll use it to feel like I'm being shown a winning lottery ticket I don't know how to cash. The screen will show me an APN (still don't fully know what that is in my gut), an assessed value, a motivation grade ("A-"), a top signal ("3 years tax delinquent"). And I'll think: **what do I do with this?** Do I buy it? Do I call somebody? Do I send a letter? Is this person about to lose their land and I'm the vulture? Is that bad? Is that the business?

There is no version of step 3 that explains to me, the human, **what to do next with the parcel I'm being shown.** That's the moment a first-timer either commits to the platform or quietly closes the tab. The "aha" that Wendell needs ("this data is real and accurate") is different from the "aha" I need ("I now understand what this business actually looks like on a Tuesday morning").

**Step 5 — Pax tour.** I get introduced to an AI named Pax who works 24/7 so I don't have to. Cool. But I haven't done any work for Pax to take off my plate yet. Pax meeting me before I have a single lead in the system is like getting introduced to my new assistant before I have a job. I don't need an assistant. I need to be told what the job is.

**Step 6 — done. Dropped on `/today`.** This is where I bounce.

`/today` is the dense surface Wendell described — Pulse score, AI actions, expiring offers, stale leads, goal bar. For Wendell with 340 leads imported, it's a firehose he can drink from. For me with zero leads imported, it should be a clean room with a single instruction. Instead I'm pretty sure I'm seeing an "empty state" with mostly placeholder copy ("you'll see expiring offers here") and the visual density of a cockpit. The sidebar has — I just counted — at least 30 nav items across CRM, Campaigns, Inbox, Pax, Insights, Finance, and more. A first-timer cannot orient inside 30 menu items. I see Map, Pipeline, Properties, Blind Offers, Skip Tracing, Dedupe, Documents, Offer Batches, Marketplace, Listings — and that's just the CRM module. **I do not know what half of those words mean in a precise enough way to click them with confidence.**

So in the first 5 minutes I have learned: AcreOS is for people who already know. I am not yet one of those people. There is a path to becoming one of those people but it's not visible from where I'm standing.

## 3. The education gap — terms I don't know

Here is a list of words and acronyms I encountered in the first 10 minutes that I either don't fully know or could not confidently define on a quiz. None of them have a hover-tooltip. None of them link to a definition. The Academy page exists and has good courses (MAO basics! Mail campaign! Due diligence checklist!) — but the Academy is a separate destination I have to *go to*. It is not woven into the surface where I'm encountering the term. That distinction matters more than it sounds. When I'm staring at the word "MAO" inside a tooltip on a parcel detail screen, I cannot leave that screen, navigate to Academy, scroll through 30 courses, find the right one, watch a 20-minute lesson, and come back to where I was. I will Google it instead. And once I'm in Google I'm two clicks from a podcast on the BiggerPockets feed and the AcreOS tab is forgotten until I close it tomorrow.

- **APN** — assessor's parcel number. I think. Is it the same as a parcel ID? Tax map number? The county uses "parcel number" on my deed but the platform says APN. Are those interchangeable? (Yes, they are. I had to confirm by searching.)
- **MAO** — Maximum Allowable Offer. Now I know because I scrolled the Academy. Before I scrolled the Academy, I saw "MAO" in a tooltip somewhere and assumed it was a typo.
- **Blind offer** — sending an offer letter at a low price to a list of owners without ever talking to them, hoping a percentage say yes. There's a `/blind-offer-wizard` in the sidebar. The word "blind" implies I'm doing it without information, which sounds reckless. It's actually a calculated wholesale technique. **Tooltip required.**
- **Skip tracing** — finding someone's contact info from public records. Sounds illegal the first time you read it. (It's not illegal.) **Tooltip required.**
- **Comping** — pulling comparable sales. **Tooltip required.**
- **DD checklist** — due diligence. I know this one. But "DD" alone is a coin-toss for first-timers.
- **1098-INT** — IRS form for interest paid on a note. I will not need this for years. But it's mentioned in pricing-faq and I don't know if I should be paying for a plan that includes it.
- **Dodd-Frank checker** — I have heard "Dodd-Frank" in a podcast but cannot tell you what it actually requires. The platform has a whole compliance checker and I don't know if I'll ever need it.
- **AVM** — Automated Valuation Model. I figured it out from context. **Tooltip would have saved me 30 seconds and a Google.**
- **Buy-box** — your investment criteria (counties, acreage, price). I learned this from podcasts. Many first-timers haven't.
- **Note / Notes / Note investor** — in normal English a note is a piece of paper. In land it's a seller-financed loan. The whole "Money / Notes" tab is opaque to me on first read.
- **Pebble, REsimpli, REI Pro, Pebble** — referenced in pricing FAQ as platforms I might be migrating from. I'm migrating from a Google Sheet. **The fact that the platform assumes I have a CSV from Pebble already tells me again: this is for Wendell.**
- **Land Credit Score** — okay this one is pitched well. The pricing FAQ explains it as "FICO for land." That's the right analogy. More of that.

**The pattern:** AcreOS uses the vocabulary of an experienced operator everywhere, and the Academy where it teaches the vocabulary is one click off the main path. For a first-timer, every page should have a discreet `?` next to terms-of-art that opens a popover with a 2-line definition and a "learn more" link. I should be able to hover any acronym and get oriented in 3 seconds without losing my place. Bonus points if the popover ends with "Was this helpful? Yes / Sort of / No" and feeds back into a beginner-mode personalization signal — Pax could note "Grace doesn't know what blind offers are yet, walk her through one before suggesting one."

There's a related issue in the *empty states*. When I look at `/leads` with zero leads in it, the empty state should not just say "No leads yet — Add lead." It should say something like "**No leads yet. Most first-time investors start by pulling a 50-owner list from one target county and sending blind offers. Want a 5-minute walkthrough?** [Yes, walk me through] [I know what I'm doing]." That single decision, on every empty-state surface, is the difference between a user who learns the platform and a user who pokes at it for an hour and leaves.

## 4. Trust signals — what's missing for a first-timer

The land investing world online is **70% scams and gurus.** I've watched 14-minute YouTube ads that turn out to be pitches for a $2,997 mentorship. I've gotten emails from "Tate's land team" about "the secret 47 motivated sellers" three times this month. My friend's sister bought a $5,000 course and got a Facebook group and a PDF.

So when I hit a new platform that says "Operating System for Land Investors," my fraud antennae are extended. Here's what's working and what isn't.

**Working:**
- The pricing page has a Free tier. Real platforms have free tiers. Scams have "limited time $1 trial that auto-bills $497."
- The pricing-faq says my data stays if I cancel. Good.
- The founder copy has Thomas's name on it. Real human, real signature. No "join thousands of millionaires." The line "I'm a land investor, and I'm the kind of person who can't leave a broken system alone" sounds like a real person, not a marketing team.
- "In private beta with 12 land investors. $1.4M closed in 90 days." This is a small, specific, plausible claim. Scams say "$50M closed." This says 12 investors and $1.4M. Believable.
- 14-day free trial, no card required. Critical. If they wanted my card before the trial I'd close.
- The pricing-faq actually answers questions instead of selling. That's a real-product signal.

**Not working:**
- "Three AI agents — Atlas, Pax, Sophie." For someone like me who's burned out on "AI-powered everything," **three named AI agents sound like a marketing flourish.** I want one assistant or zero assistants. Three feels like the team is leaning into the AI angle to compete with VC-funded competitors. (It also makes me wonder where the other two agents are — the landing copy says three but the in-app rail mostly shows Pax. If it's really three, why am I only meeting one?)
- "60-hour week into a 20-hour one." This is the aspirational hero copy. For me, I'm not working a 60-hour land week. I'm working a 4-hour land week on Saturday afternoon. The promise of "20 hours" makes me think the platform assumes I have 20 hours.
- Beta testimonial absence on the in-app surfaces. The landing has them (good). Once I'm signed up the testimonials stop. I'd want a small "see how others started" sidebar pointing to the Academy + a couple of beta-user mini-stories.
- No visible "AcreOS Promise" or warranty. If the note ledger miscounts, what happens? Are my docs encrypted? Where's the data? **For a first-timer, "I'm trusting you with my $14K asset's records" needs a more explicit reassurance than "field-level encryption (AES-256)" buried in FAQ #8.** A "your data, your privacy, our promise" page in plain English would help.
- No human in the loop. I want to know there's someone I can email with a question and a real human will reply within a day. The platform has Pax, but Pax is an AI. I want **Sarah at AcreOS support** in my inbox. The pricing matrix shows "Priority support" only on Pro+, which implies non-priority support exists for free + starter, but I can't find a "contact us" link from the pricing page that doesn't make me feel like I'm bothering somebody.

## 5. Pricing fit — too expensive? Wrong tier?

Let me be honest about the math.

I am going to do, optimistically, **2 deals this year.** Each deal will net me $3K-$7K. Total likely income from land in 2026: **$8,000-$14,000.** Software cost as a fraction of that:

- **Free** ($0/yr): 0%. Acceptable. But the limits — 10 leads, 3 properties, 25 AI requests/day — are tight enough that I'll hit them by month 2. (I have 41 saved listings already. Free tier caps at 10. Done.)
- **Starter** ($240/yr): 1.7%-3% of revenue. Acceptable. The 250-lead, 50-property, 25-note caps are massive overkill for me. I'd never use them. But I'm paying for headroom I don't need.
- **Pro** ($588/yr): 4.2%-7.4%. Edge. Justifiable only if Pro unlocks something Starter doesn't that I genuinely need.
- **Scale** ($948/yr): 6.8%-12%. Not happening.

**The problem with the current pricing structure for first-timers:**

1. **The Free tier is a cliff, not a ramp.** 10 leads is too few — anyone scrolling Zillow for a weekend has 10 leads. I'll outgrow Free in week one and feel pressured into a paid plan before I've validated whether AcreOS works for me.

2. **There's no "true beginner" tier.** What I want is a "**Hobbyist**" tier at maybe $9/mo with 50 leads, 10 properties, 5 notes, no SMS, no team, no campaigns — everything I need for one or two deals a year and nothing I don't. Right now Starter at $20 gives me 250 leads (don't need), 50 properties (don't need), 25 notes (definitely don't need). I'm being asked to pay for Wendell's volume.

3. **The trial is generous but landed wrong.** Pricing-faq #5 says it's 90 days of full Pro. The pricing page says 14 days. Those are different. (Resolve that copy mismatch — I noticed it in 3 minutes.) Either way, 90 days of Pro will let me do a deal start-to-finish on the platform — *if* I know how to do a deal, which I don't. **A 90-day Pro trial wasted on a confused first-timer is a 90-day Pro trial wasted.** I need a beginner-mode that tutorial-walks me through the trial.

4. **Per-deal pricing would convert me.** If AcreOS said "free up to your first deal, then $99 per closed deal, billed at close," I'd sign up tonight. That maps software cost to my actual revenue. SaaS subscription pricing assumes a steady volume; I don't have steady volume yet. (I know per-deal pricing is hard to build. I know it's not in the cards. But it's the price model that fits me.)

**My recommendation:** Add a "Hobbyist" tier at $9/mo with conservative limits, position it explicitly as "Your first 1-3 deals." Then Starter becomes "Your active 5-10 deal/year practice." Then Pro becomes Wendell. Three tiers that map to three real life stages, not four tiers that all assume operator volume.

## 6. The deal-killer for a first-timer

Wendell's deal-killer is the note ledger. If it miscounts once, he's gone.

**My deal-killer is the empty Tuesday morning.**

Here's what I mean. I sign up for AcreOS. I do the onboarding. I get dropped on `/today`. There are 30 menu items in the sidebar. There's an AI assistant offering to help. There's a Pulse score I don't understand. There are at least four floating CTAs (the help button, the Pax button, the command palette hint, a notification bell).

It's a Saturday. I have 4 hours. **I do not know what to do with those 4 hours inside this software.**

That's the killer. Not a bug. Not a missing feature. The absence of a single, narrated, unambiguous **"Here's what to do for the next 30 minutes, and here's the deal you'll have started by the end of it"** experience.

What would save me — what would convert me from "tab open" to "paying customer who tells her three real-estate-curious friends" — is a **First Deal Workspace.** Not the onboarding wizard. A persistent surface that lives in the sidebar at the top (above all the operator menu items) for as long as my org has zero closed deals, that says:

> **Your first deal**
> Step 3 of 12: Send your first blind-offer letter
> *Estimated: 25 min · Why we're doing this: [tooltip]*
> [ Continue ]

And it walks me through, in order:
1. Pick a target county (with budget/radius helper, not just a dropdown)
2. Pull a 50-owner list (auto, with explanation)
3. Set my MAO using the calculator (with definitions)
4. Generate a blind-offer letter (template, edited, explained)
5. Send mail to 50 owners (real, $1.50 a piece, explain return rate expectations)
6. Wait for replies (this step is "wait 14 days, here's what to do meanwhile")
7. Negotiate first reply (script + Pax draft)
8. Run due diligence (the existing checklist, narrated)
9. Sign purchase agreement (e-sign flow)
10. Close (escrow walkthrough)
11. List for resale (Facebook Marketplace + Land.com walkthroughs)
12. Sell — and then this whole UI disappears and I get the operator surface.

That is **the missing product.** And it's not just for me. It's the difference between AcreOS having a TAM of "experienced land investors who already know what they're doing" — which is small — and AcreOS having a TAM of "anyone who heard the BiggerPockets episode on land and Googled how to start" — which is enormous.

Wendell looks at the platform and says "the notes have to be right." He's right.

I look at the platform and say **"the first deal has to be possible."** Right now it's possible if you bring your own knowledge. It's not possible if you don't.

## 7. Mistakes I'd make without prompts

Things I would get wrong inside AcreOS in my first month, that an operator wouldn't:

- **I'd send blind offers to my own town's county.** The wizard would let me. Nothing stops me from picking Ada County, ID — where prices are too high for blind offers to work — and sending 50 letters that get zero responses because my MAO is laughable to suburban Boise owners. **A one-line warning ("Ada County's median land price is $X. Blind-offer success rates are typically below 1% above $Y. Consider a more rural county.") would save me $75 of mail and a week of demoralization.**
- **I'd skip due diligence and overpay.** I'd see a 5-acre lot listed at $8K and think "that's a deal!" without checking flood zone, road access, or buildability. The DD checklist exists. But unless the platform *insists* I run it before I send a purchase offer — gates the offer button on it, even — I'll skip it because I'm excited.
- **I'd misclassify my one rental income as ordinary income at tax time.** Then end up amending. The platform has a "Tax pack" — but I don't know to use it.
- **I'd talk to a seller on the phone without a script and say something stupid.** Like "what's your lowest price?" right out the gate, which kills the negotiation. The Academy mentions scripts. The platform has Pax. Neither one offers me a "first phone call helper" the moment I click "log call" on a lead.
- **I'd forget to verify the deed before sending money.** The platform pulls assessor data. I'd assume the assessor data is the deed. They are not the same. A first-timer doesn't know that.

Each of these is a real first-timer mistake that would cost me $200-$5,000. Each is preventable with a single contextual nudge at the right moment in the workflow. None of those nudges exist today, as far as I can tell.

## 8. The "I'm overwhelmed" path

Final piece. When I'm overwhelmed, what do I do?

Right now there's no "I'm overwhelmed" button. There's Pax, but Pax is the AI and Pax assumes I have something specific to ask. There's the help panel, which is a list of feature guides — also assumes I know what I'm looking for. There's the Academy, which is courses — assumes I have 25 minutes.

What I want when I'm overwhelmed is a single button labeled **"I'm stuck"** that opens a panel with three options: (1) "Show me what I should be doing right now" — surfaces the next step in my First Deal Workspace. (2) "Connect me to a real human at AcreOS" — books a 15-min call with someone, even if it's only available 1 day a week. (3) "Hide everything I'm not using" — collapses the sidebar to just the 3-4 surfaces a first-timer needs. Pipeline, Properties, Inbox, Today. Hide everything else until I ask for it.

The third option is critical. My sidebar today has 30+ items. I'm a software engineer and I find that overwhelming. Somebody who came here from a Facebook group will not survive it. **A "First-Timer Mode" toggle in settings that hides Marketplace, Capital Markets, Acquisition Radar, Land Credit, Compliance, Dodd-Frank Checker, Document Versions, Cohort Analysis, Beta Analytics, and the rest of the operator surfaces** — until I've closed my first deal — would do more for my activation than any new feature you could ship.

## Closing thought

I want to like this. The Academy courses are well-written. The founder letter sounds like a real person. The HMAC e-sign architecture (which I had to look up because Wendell mentioned it) is apparently very good. The free government data sources (FEMA, USDA, Census — 18 of them!) is the kind of thing I would never have thought to use, and it's exactly the kind of due-diligence muscle a first-timer is missing.

But all of that is locked behind the assumption that I already know what I'm doing. I don't. And the platform doesn't yet have the patience to teach me, woven into the actual surfaces, in the moment, while I'm trying.

If you build the First Deal Workspace, add a Hobbyist tier, drop tooltips on every term-of-art, and add a real-human "ask us anything" channel that doesn't make me feel like I'm bothering Pax — I'll sign up Saturday and tell my book club.

Until then, I'm closing the tab and going back to the spreadsheet. Which is the saddest churn there is, because I never actually used anything.

— Grace
