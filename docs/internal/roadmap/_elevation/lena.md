# Lena — CFO/CIO — Elevation Brief (2026-06-07)

**Lens:** Financial machinery elevation — pricing/packaging, credit-pool + COGS instrumentation, founder finance cockpit depth, unit-economics, billing robustness, the path-to-paid-data trigger. Both sides.

**Posture before I start:** the financial *substrate* is genuinely good now. Revenue actually splits into the five allocation buckets on the Stripe webhook (`server/webhookHandlers.ts:760`), Stripe fees post as opex (`:878`), the daily→monthly Pax-cap margin leak is fixed (`server/services/usageLimits.ts` `getMonthlyAiRequestCount`), credit weights track p90 provider cost (`shared/billing/credit-weights.ts`), paid-data is gated behind a 2%-of-MRR + $200-MRR phase rule (`shared/billing/data-procurement-policy.ts`), customer unit economics roll up nightly with an unprofitable-streak alarm (`server/services/unitEconomics.ts`), and there's a no-spend buy-decision harness for paid data (`server/routes-founder-paid-data-eval.ts`). That is a strong base.

The gap between "good substrate" and "best-in-class CFO machinery" is **synthesis and decision-grade output**: the numbers exist but they don't yet *add up to a position*. There is no runway-to-zero model, no three-scenario forecast, no LTV:CAC/payback, no continuous finance-audit loop — the exact disciplines my charter says are the standard. That's where the elevation is.

## Top ideas (highest value first)

### 1. The runway-to-zero engine — three scenarios, one number. `develop` · founder · L
**Why it matters / what "great" looks like.** My charter names runway-to-zero as "the single most-watched number after MRR," refreshed weekly, always in base/downside/upside. Today it does not exist as a model. `GET /api/founder/money/summary` divides a hand-typed `FOUNDER_CASH_ON_HAND_USD` env var by a crude 30-day burn (`server/routes-founder-money.ts:83-94`) and calls it runway. That is a self-reported placeholder, not a model — and the doc comment admits it ("Phase 1 (Lena) replaces the env-configurable knobs"). Great looks like: a `runwayModel.ts` service that reads real burn from `financial_ledger` (opex_spent rows) + `solene_capital_events` + the fixed-cost inputs, projects forward 18 months under three named scenarios (base / downside = revenue flat + costs +20% / upside = trajectory continues), and returns a months-to-zero per scenario with the trend delta vs. last week. This is the spine everything else hangs off.
**First step.** Create `server/services/finance/runwayModel.ts` exporting `computeRunway(): RunwayResult` with `scenarios: { base, downside, upside }`. Pull burn from `financialLedger` opex (reuse `getBucketBalance`/`getContributionMargin` in `server/services/financial-ledger.ts`), cash from the `profit_reserve` + `opex_available` bucket balances (real, not env), MRR from `tierForSubscriptionTier`. Wire it into `routes-founder-money.ts` summary, keeping the env value only as a labeled "founder-declared override."

### 2. The 7-detector finance-audit loop — the continuous-monitoring infrastructure I'm supposed to own. `develop` · founder · L
**Why it matters.** My brief specifies this loop at `server/services/lena/financeAudit.ts` with seven named detectors (envelope overrun, vendor-cost drift, tax-reserve shortfall, runway crunch, founder-draw miss, mission-transfer miss, unit-econ regression). It does not exist — the `server/services/lena/` directory isn't there. Right now money problems surface only via the one nightly `customer_unprofitable` alert (`unitEconomics.ts:396`). A best-in-class CFO function detects drift *before* the founder asks. Great looks like: a nightly + weekly detector pass that writes to a `lena_audit_findings` table and pages Solene severity-high on money signals.
**First step.** Create `server/services/lena/financeAudit.ts` with the three detectors that have real data *today* — envelope overrun (against `solene_capital_events` + `DEFAULT_MONTHLY_ENVELOPE_USD`), tax-reserve shortfall (bucket balance vs 25% of trailing revenue), and runway crunch (depends on #1). Stub the four that need post-launch data. Schedule via `server/jobs/runScheduledJobs.ts`.

### 3. LTV : CAC : payback — the gate metrics, modeled and surfaced. `develop` · both · M
**Why it matters.** The charter's elite-finance bar is explicit: CAC payback < 12 months at Phase 2, LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1 at Phase 3, gross margin ≥ 70% sustained. None of these three are computed anywhere — `getContributionMargin` exists (`financial-ledger.ts:343`) but nothing turns it into lifetime value, and CAC isn't tracked at all (Soren ships campaigns; the cost→customer attribution loop my charter says I own with him is missing). The day the first customer lands we will be asked "what's our payback?" and have no answer. Great looks like: a unit-economics page section that shows blended gross margin (already computable from `readUnitEconomicsRollup`), contribution margin per cohort, and — once CAC inputs exist — LTV (contribution margin × expected lifetime from churn) over CAC, with the phase-gate thresholds drawn as reference lines.
**First step.** Extend `UnitEconomicsApiResponse` (`unitEconomics.ts:510`) with `grossMarginPct` trend (have it) + a `cohortContribution` block keyed by signup-month, using `getContributionMargin`. Add a `marketing_spend` ledger feature so CAC has a numerator; surface "LTV:CAC = N/A (no CAC data yet)" honestly rather than faking it.

### 4. Pricing/packaging: decide the structure and ship the credit-recharge economics. `improve` · both · M
**Why it matters.** I already wrote `docs/internal/pricing/alternatives-2026-06-06.md` with three structures and honest unit economics — but it's pre-decisional and no structure is chosen. Meanwhile a live margin question is unresolved: the customer **credit recharge packs** advertise "$20 → ~2,000 credits" (`client/src/pages/outreach/mail/credits.tsx:44-49`), i.e. 1 credit = 1¢ at par — we resell provider cost at cost, zero margin on recharge. That's a deliberate choice or a leak; either way it should be named. And Scale's `ai_requests` is now capped at 50,000/mo (good) but the credit pool (8,000) and the AI cap aren't reconciled — a Scale user can run 50k turns (≈$750 COGS) while the 8k pool only covers a fraction, because AI turns draw the pool too. Great looks like: a one-page "pricing decision memo v2" that (a) recommends one structure, (b) sets a recharge markup (even 15–20% keeps it honest and contribution-positive), (c) reconciles each tier's AI cap × blended cost against its pool + sticker so no tier can go contribution-negative at the cap.
**First step.** Add a "margin-at-cap" column to the alternatives doc per tier: `(sticker − ai_cap × $0.015 − pool_value − fixed_share)`. Flag any negative. Then recommend recharge markup in `PACKS` and the matching credit-grant math.

### 5. Reconcile the credit pool against AI turns — close the double-count / under-count seam. `refine` · customer · M
**Why it matters.** AI turns now draw the pool (`creditPool.ts` maps `ai_turn_avg → "ai_tokens"` bucket) AND the `ai_requests` monthly quota gates turns separately. Two independent meters for the same action means a customer can be quota-OK but pool-empty, or pool-OK but quota-blocked — confusing, and the relationship between "Pax messages / month" and "credits" is never explained to the customer. The credits page lists `ai_tokens` as a category but the customer has no idea a Pax turn spends ~1.5 credits. Great looks like: one coherent story — "your tier includes N Pax messages; heavy data lookups and outreach draw your credit pool" — with the two meters visibly distinct and non-overlapping, or AI folded entirely into one or the other.
**First step.** Decide: does an AI turn draw the pool, the quota, or both? My recommendation — quota gates Pax messages (it's the headline number customers buy on), pool covers *marginal provider spend* (mail/SMS/skip-trace/paid-data). Remove `ai_turn_avg` from the default pool-debit path or make its pool draw explicit in the credits UI. Touch `creditPool.ts:41` mapping + `credits.tsx` copy.

### 6. The path-to-paid-data trigger: make it a live, automatic recommendation. `elevate` · founder · M
**Why it matters.** The gate logic is excellent (`evaluatePaidProviderGate`) and the eval harness is excellent (`paid-data-eval`), but they're disconnected and manual — the founder has to remember to go run the eval and separately check whether MRR cleared $200. A best-in-class system *tells* you when the buy becomes rational. Great looks like: a single "Paid Data Readiness" card on the money cockpit that shows, live: trailing MRR vs the $200 gate, the 2%-of-MRR commit ceiling, the latest eval's decision-flip count, and a green "Regrid pay-per-call is now justified — N% of worked parcels would flip a decision, gate cleared" verdict the moment all conditions are met. This converts a deferred decision into an automatic trigger — exactly the "MRR-gated real paid-data purchase" the founder deferred, made self-announcing.
**First step.** New `GET /api/founder/paid-data-eval/readiness` combining `evaluatePaidProviderGate` (fed real trailing MRR from the ledger) + `getLatestEvalRun` + per-provider `minMonthlyCommitCents`. Render as a card on `client/src/pages/founder/money.tsx`.

### 7. Tax-reserve & quarterly-estimate integrity: prove the buckets hold. `improve` · founder · S
**Why it matters.** Revenue splits 25% to `tax_reserve` on every invoice, and there's a quarterly-estimate radar + 1040/MA-Form-1 draft engine shipped. But there's no continuous check that the reserve *balance* actually stays ≥ 25% of trailing-12-month revenue — my charter's hard rule ("cannot let the tax reserve fall below 25%"). The buckets are accounting fictions until something reconciles their summed balance against the rule and against what the draft engine says is owed. Great looks like: the tax-reserve detector from #2, plus a money-cockpit line "Tax reserve: $X (covers Y% of est. liability)" that ties the bucket balance to the draft engine's computed number.
**First step.** Add `getBucketBalance("tax_reserve")` vs `0.25 × trailing12Revenue` as detector #3 in `financeAudit.ts`; surface the coverage ratio on the money page using the existing draft-engine estimate.

## Boldest elevation bet

**The Founder Finance Cockpit as a true CFO co-pilot — one page that states a position, not six pages that show numbers.**

Right now finance is scattered across `money.tsx`, `unit-economics.tsx`, `cost.tsx`, `ai-costs.tsx`, `cost-optimizer.tsx`, `observability-cost.tsx` plus a dozen `routes-founder-*` endpoints. That's six dashboards and zero conclusions. The bold bet is to build the thing my charter actually describes: a single cockpit that, every morning, answers the four questions a real CFO answers without being asked — **"How many months of runway, in three scenarios? Is any unit going contribution-negative? Is any reserve below its floor? What's the one money decision in front of you this week?"** — and writes that synthesis as a short, dated narrative ("Runway 14mo base / 9mo downside, stable WoW. All customers contribution-positive. Tax reserve covers 110% of est. liability. Decision pending: paid-data gate clears at $200 MRR — you're at $0.").

This is the difference between AcreOS *having* financial data and AcreOS *having a CFO*. It's the synthesis layer (#1, #2, #3 feeding a narrative generator) that makes Tom never have to assemble the picture himself — which is the whole point of the role. Built right, it's also the "data room ready in 5 days" posture my Tranche 4 calls for, as a byproduct.

## Small high-ROI polish refinements

- **Bucket-balance integrity invariant.** Add a cheap nightly assertion that `sum(all five bucket balances) == sum(all revenue rows) − sum(all opex/refund rows)`. There's a `financial-ledger-invariant.ts` already; confirm it covers cross-bucket conservation, not just per-row signs.
- **`monthlyPayment` rough-average leak.** `financeEnhancements.ts:97` estimates expected collections as `noteCount × 500` ("rough average") — that's a fabricated number rendered to a customer in collection-rate %. Replace with the sum of actual `note.monthlyPayment`. This violates the truth-engine discipline.
- **Calendar `as any` status cast.** `financeEnhancements.ts:35` casts the payment status through `as any` — tighten the type; it's a `PaymentStatus` union.
- **Round honest, not flattering.** Audit `unitEconomics.ts` `profitMarginPct` and the cockpit numbers for any rounding that flatters margin upward. My hard rule: never overstate an internal number.
- **Fail-open vs fail-loud on pool debit.** `creditPool.ts:222` fails *open* (never block a customer if the ledger errors) — correct for UX, but there's no counter/alert when it happens. A silent fail-open is a silent margin leak. Emit a metric.
- **Recharge-pack credit math display.** The packs say "~2,000 credits" for $20 — make the "~" honest by showing the actual grant after any markup decision (#4), so the customer-facing number matches the ledger.
- **Stale pricing-doc divisor note.** `tier-limits.ts` comments still narrate the old 500/day → 1500/mo migration in detail; once the structure is decided (#4), collapse the historical commentary so the file reads as current truth.

## The one thing that would most embarrass us if a sharp first customer (or I) noticed it unpolished

**The founder runway is a hand-typed env var dressed up as a model, and there is no three-scenario forecast anywhere — for a product whose entire premise is honest, sourced numbers.**

If the first customer is the kind of land investor who reads a P&L (many are — they underwrite notes for a living), and they ever glimpse that our *own* "runway" comes from `FOUNDER_CASH_ON_HAND_USD` divided by a 30-day average, while we sell them a platform built on a provenance contract that refuses to show a number without a source — that's a credibility puncture. Worse, it would embarrass *me*: my charter opens with "models 90 days ahead, plans 12 months ahead, three scenarios always," and the disqualifier is literally "a forecast without the three scenarios." We don't have one. The substrate to build it real exists today (the ledger has the burn, the buckets have the cash). Not building it is the gap. That's why #1 leads this list.
