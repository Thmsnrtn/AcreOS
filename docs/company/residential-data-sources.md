### Explicitly NOT free/open (common misconceptions)

- **MLS / RESO Web API**: the RESO *standard* is open; the *data* is not. Access requires per-MLS data license agreements, typically involving a participating broker and MLS approval ([reso.org/reso-web-api](https://www.reso.org/reso-web-api/), [MLS Grid example](https://kcrar.com/kcrar-members/heartland-mls/data-feeds-and-vendor-inquiries/)). MLS sold data is also the only reliable comp source in non-disclosure states.
- **Zillow Bridge API**: Zillow's public API (ZWSID) was retired Sept 30, 2021; Bridge is for MLS members/approved partners only, and the Zestimate API is partner-only ([bridgeinteractive.com](https://www.bridgeinteractive.com/developers/zillow-group-data/), [estated explainer](https://blog.estated.com/what-happened-to-zillows-api)).
- **ATTOM**: paid, custom-quoted bulk/API licensing (entry tool Property Navigator is $499/yr) ([attomdata.com bulk licensing](https://www.attomdata.com/solutions/bulk-data-licensing/), [pricing](https://www.attomdata.com/solutions/property-navigator/pricing/)).

### Non-disclosure states

Sale prices are not public record in roughly 12 states: **AK, ID, KS, LA, MS, MO (partial), MT, NM, ND, TX, UT, WY** ([retipster map](https://retipster.com/non-disclosure-states-map/), [homelight](https://www.homelight.com/blog/non-disclosure-states-real-estate/)). In these states no county open-data file will contain prices; MLS access is the only lawful comp source. Utah suppresses prices even beyond public records ([homelight](https://www.homelight.com/blog/non-disclosure-states-real-estate/)).

## (b) What this means for house-flip comps

**A free stack cannot honestly power per-property residential comps nationally.** The only genuinely open property-level sales data is a patchwork of disclosure-state county/state files (NYC, Cook, King, Philadelphia, CT, VT, MD, upstate NY, plus dozens of similar portals), each with its own schema, lag (monthly–quarterly typical; Vermont is weekly), and no standardized "arm's-length" flagging. Twelve states publish no prices at all, and the national area-index sources (Zillow, Redfin) are (1) aggregates, not comps, and (2) legally gray for SaaS embedding. Anyone claiming free nationwide comps is reselling scraped or licensed data.

What a free stack **can** honestly power:

1. **A market-context layer, nationally**: FHFA HPI (public domain — monthly metro/state, annual ZIP5/tract) + Census ACS values + HUD rents give trend, appreciation, and rent-context at ZIP/tract level with clean licensing. This supports "is this market heating/cooling," ARV sanity ranges, and hold-period appreciation assumptions — not per-address comps.
2. **A disclosure-state county ETL program**: real property-level comps in the specific metros above, built county-by-county, with per-portal license checks. This is genuinely valuable in those markets and honest to market as "comps available in supported counties."
3. **HMDA as a coarse price proxy** in non-disclosure states (tract-level, privacy-rounded, financed sales only) — useful for modeling, never presentable as a comp.

## (c) Recommendation

Build the free tier as a two-layer system: a nationwide market-context layer on FHFA HPI + ACS + HUD (all public domain, safe in a commercial SaaS with attribution), and a per-county property-level sales ETL starting with NYC, Cook, King, Philadelphia, and the CT/VT/MD/NY statewide files, expanding portal-by-portal with license verification. Do not embed Zillow ZHVI or Redfin aggregates in the product without written permission (Zillow) or counsel sign-off on the attribution-only basis (Redfin) — their TOS for SaaS redistribution range from unclear to prohibitive. For true per-property comps everywhere (especially TX/UT/ID and the other nine non-disclosure states), budget for licensed data — MLS/RESO agreements or a vendor like ATTOM — and market the free stack honestly as market context plus supported-county comps.
