# Backstory

Dana Cho runs a tight operation out of a converted garage office behind her house in Midland. Two monitors, a standing desk, a whiteboard with county names and deal counts written in dry-erase marker. She wholesales vacant land across West Texas and parts of New Mexico, Arizona, and Colorado. Forty to fifty deals a year. She has a VA in the Philippines who skips-traces owners and sends blind offers, and a local title company that knows her by first name.

She doesn't need to be sold on land investing. She's already in the business. What she needs is a tool that keeps up with her pace. Her current stack is PropStream for data pulls, a Google Sheet that's become a monster, and Podio for her pipeline. It works, but it's duct tape. She loses deals in the gaps — a parcel she pulled data on but forgot to send an offer, an owner who called back and nobody logged it, a comp she ran manually that was already stale by the time she used it.

Dana is skeptical of AI. Not because she doesn't understand it — she's technically sharp — but because she's seen too many products slap "AI-powered" on a basic filter and call it innovation. If AcreOS tells her a parcel is worth $12,000 and she knows from experience it's a $6,000 lot, she'll write off the entire platform. She will actively test the AI to see if it's real or marketing.

She evaluates tools the way she evaluates deals: fast. She'll give AcreOS fifteen minutes. In those fifteen minutes, she wants to pull a list for Reeves County, filter by acreage and last sale date, see comps, and get a rough sense of whether the data is better, worse, or the same as what she already has. If it's the same, there's no reason to switch. If it's worse, she's gone. It has to be noticeably better.

The things she'd say out loud while using AcreOS:

"Okay, where's the list builder? I don't need a tour, I need to pull Reeves County, 5 to 40 acres, last sold more than 3 years ago."

"Why can't I sort by this column? Every other tool lets me sort by this column."

"These comps are... actually not bad. Let me cross-check a couple against what I have. If these hold up, okay, I'm listening."

"Can I bulk-tag these? I need to mark 200 parcels as 'offer sent' without clicking each one."

"Don't show me an AI confidence score if you can't explain how you got there. That's worse than no score at all."

"If I can't get my VA set up with her own login in the next two minutes, this isn't going to work for my operation."

Dana doesn't do free trials that require a credit card. She doesn't do onboarding calls. She doesn't watch tutorial videos. She reads nothing — she clicks and expects the UI to be self-evident. If a button's label is ambiguous, that's a product failure in her eyes, not a training gap. She's not rude about it, she just closes the tab and goes back to PropStream.

Her breaking point is any moment where the tool feels slower than her spreadsheet. She's built muscle memory around Ctrl+F, column sorts, and quick filters. If AcreOS makes her wait for a server round-trip to do something she can do locally in Excel, she'll feel it immediately, and it'll color her entire impression of the product.
